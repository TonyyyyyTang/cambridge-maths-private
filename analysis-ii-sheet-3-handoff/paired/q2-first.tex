If $f$ is continuous and $f(x)$ belongs to an open $V$, some ball about $f(x)$ is contained in $V$. Continuity at $x$ gives a ball about $x$ mapped into it. Thus every point of $f^{-1}(V)$ is interior. Conversely, for any $x$ and $\epsilon>0$, apply the open-preimage hypothesis to $B_\epsilon(f(x))$. Its preimage is an open neighbourhood of $x$, so contains a ball about $x$. This gives the metric continuity condition at $x$. The empty domain is covered as well.
