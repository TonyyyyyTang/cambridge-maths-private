Suppose $f$ is continuous, let $V$ be open in $X'$ and take $x\in f^{-1}(V)$. Some $\epsilon>0$ satisfies $B_{d'}(f(x),\epsilon)\subseteq V$. Continuity at $x$ supplies $\delta>0$ such that $f(B_d(x,\delta))\subseteq B_{d'}(f(x),\epsilon)$. Thus every point of $f^{-1}(V)$ has a ball in it, proving openness.

Conversely assume every open set has open inverse image. Given $x\in X$ and $\epsilon>0$, the inverse image of the open ball $B_{d'}(f(x),\epsilon)$ is open and contains $x$. It therefore contains $B_d(x,\delta)$ for some $\delta>0$, which is precisely the continuity condition at $x$. This applies at every point.
