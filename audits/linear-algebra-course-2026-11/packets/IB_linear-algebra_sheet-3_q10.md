## Question (verbatim question.tex)
```latex
Let $V$ be a vector space of dimension $n$ and $\alpha$ an endomorphism of $V$ with $\alpha^n=0$ but $\alpha^{n-1}\ne0$. Without appealing to Jordan Normal Form, show that there is a vector $y$ such that $(y,\alpha(y),\alpha^2(y),\ldots,\alpha^{n-1}(y))$ is a basis for $V$. What is the matrix representation of $\alpha$ with respect to this basis? And the matrix representation of $\alpha^k$, for an arbitrary positive integer $k$?

Show that if $\beta$ is an endomorphism of $V$ which commutes with $\alpha$, then $\beta=p(\alpha)$ for some polynomial $p$. \emph{[Hint: consider $\beta(y)$.]} What is the form of the matrix for $\beta$ with respect to the above basis?
```
