## Question (verbatim question.tex)
```latex
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space with inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Define the \emph{dual space} $V^*$ and the \emph{dual basis} associated with a basis $\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}$ of $V$. Prove that the map $\phi:V\to V^*$ given by $\phi(u)(v)=\langle u,v\rangle$ is an isomorphism between $V$ and $V^*$.
\item[(ii)] Prove that the map $\psi:V\to V^{**}$ defined by $\psi(v)(\phi)=\phi(v)$ is an isomorphism between $V$ and $V^{**}$.
\item[(iii)] Define the \emph{adjoint} $T^*$ of a linear operator $T:V\to V$. Prove that, for every linear operator $T$, the adjoint $T^*$ exists and is unique.
\end{enumerate}
\item[(b)] Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space, and let $T:V\to V$ be a linear operator.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Prove that if $T$ is diagonalisable, then there exists an inner product on $V$ with respect to which $T$ is self-adjoint.
\item[(ii)] We say that a set $S\subset V$ is $T$-invariant if $T(S)\subset S$. Prove that if $T:V\to V$ is self-adjoint with respect to some inner product, then every $T$-invariant subspace has a $T$-invariant orthogonal complement.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
```
