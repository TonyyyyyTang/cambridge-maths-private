## Question (verbatim question.tex)
```latex
Consider a particle in one dimension in a potential $U(x)$ that tends to zero rapidly as $x\to\pm\infty$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Let $\chi_1(x)$ and $\chi_2(x)$ be normalisable energy eigenfunctions of the Hamiltonian with the same energy eigenvalue $E$. By considering $\chi_1\chi_2'-\chi_2\chi_1'$, show that $\chi_1$ and $\chi_2$ must be proportional to one another. What does this mean, physically?
\item[(ii)] Can the wavefunction for a normalised energy eigenstate always be chosen to be real?
\item[(iii)] Show that if $\chi(x)$ is any normalised energy eigenstate then $\langle\hat p\rangle_\chi=0$.
\end{enumerate}
```
