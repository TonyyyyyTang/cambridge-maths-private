## Question (verbatim question.tex)
```latex
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] A particle of mass $\mu$ moves in a spherically symmetric potential $U(r)$ in three dimensions. Show that $\chi(\mathbf x)=R(r)Y(\theta,\varphi)$ is an energy eigenstate iff (i) $Y(\theta,\varphi)$ is an eigenfunction of $\hat L^2$, with eigenvalue $\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)$, say, and (ii) $\chi(r)=rR(r)$ satisfies the radial Schrödinger equation with effective potential
\[
U(r)+\frac{\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)}{2\mu r^2}\,.
\]
[\emph{You may quote properties of $\hat L^2$ that you need, including its relation to $\nabla^2$.}]
\item[(b)] A three-dimensional oscillator of mass $\mu$ is governed by the potential $U(r)=\frac12\mu\omega^2r^2$.

Setting $\ell=0$ in part (a), deduce the energy levels of the oscillator for this value of the angular momentum. [\emph{You may quote results for a one-dimensional oscillator.}]

For general $\ell$, re-express the radial Schrödinger equation for $\chi(r)$ as a differential equation for $f(y)$, where $\chi(r)=f(y)\,e^{-y^2/2}$ and $y=(\mu\omega/\hbar)^{1/2}r$. Find all solutions of the form $f(y)=Cy^\alpha$ and determine the corresponding energy eigenvalues (you need not calculate $C$).
\item[(c)*] \emph{If time permits} : Use a power series approach to find all solutions for $f(y)$ in part (b) that give normalisable energy eigenstates. What are the allowed values of $\ell$ at each energy?
\end{enumerate}
```
