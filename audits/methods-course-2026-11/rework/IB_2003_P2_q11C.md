## Full Solution

We work in three-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^3$ with orthonormal Cartesian coordinates $x_i$ ($i=1,2,3$) and standard Einstein summation convention over repeated indices.

### 1. Transformation law for an $n$th-rank Cartesian tensor

Let the Cartesian coordinate axes undergo an orthogonal transformation (change of orthonormal basis) $x'_i = R_{ip} x_p$, where $R$ is an orthogonal matrix satisfying
\[
R_{ip} R_{jp} = \delta_{ij} \qquad \text{and} \qquad R_{pi} R_{pj} = \delta_{ij} \qquad (R R^T = R^T R = I),
\]
(for proper rotations, $R \in SO(3)$ with $\det R = +1$; for true/polar tensors under all orthogonal transformations, $R \in O(3)$ with $\det R = \pm 1$). An array $T_{ij\cdots k}$ with $n$ indices is an $n$th-rank Cartesian tensor if its components transform under every such change of axes as
\[
T'_{ij\cdots k} = R_{ip} R_{jq} \cdots R_{kr} \, T_{pq\cdots r},
\]
with $n$ factors of $R$ and summation over each of the $n$ dummy indices $p, q, \dots, r \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.

### 2. Isotropy of $c_{ijkl} = \alpha \delta_{ij}\delta_{kl} + \beta \delta_{ik}\delta_{jl} + \gamma \delta_{il}\delta_{jk}$

A tensor is *isotropic* if its components are unchanged under every orthogonal transformation $R$, i.e. $c'_{ijkl} = c_{ijkl}$. Applying the fourth-rank tensor transformation law to $c_{pqrs}$ (with scalars $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ invariant):
\[
\begin{aligned}
c'_{ijkl}
&= R_{ip} R_{jq} R_{kr} R_{ls} \left(\alpha \delta_{pq}\delta_{rs} + \beta \delta_{pr}\delta_{qs} + \gamma \delta_{ps}\delta_{qr}\right) \\
&= \alpha (R_{ip} R_{jq} \delta_{pq})(R_{kr} R_{ls} \delta_{rs})
 + \beta (R_{ip} R_{kr} \delta_{pr})(R_{jq} R_{ls} \delta_{qs})
 + \gamma (R_{ip} R_{ls} \delta_{ps})(R_{jq} R_{kr} \delta_{qr}).
\end{aligned}
\]
By orthogonality of $R$, for any indices $a, b \in \{1,2,3\}$,
\[
R_{au} R_{bv} \delta_{uv} = R_{au} R_{bu} = (R R^T)_{ab} = \delta_{ab}.
\]
Applying this identity to all six parenthesised contractions gives
\[
c'_{ijkl} = \alpha \delta_{ij}\delta_{kl} + \beta \delta_{ik}\delta_{jl} + \gamma \delta_{il}\delta_{jk} = c_{ijkl}
\]
for every orthogonal matrix $R \in O(3)$ and all arbitrary scalars $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$. Hence $c_{ijkl}$ is an isotropic fourth-rank tensor.

### 3. Stress-strain relationship in an isotropic linear elastic medium

Because the medium is isotropic, its fourth-rank elasticity tensor $c_{ijkl}$ must be isotropic. We first recall and prove that in $\mathbb{R}^3$ *every* isotropic fourth-rank tensor has the form $c_{ijkl} = \alpha\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl} + \beta\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl} + \gamma\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}$:

- **Proof of the general form:**
  1. Under the proper rotation $R = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1, -1) \in SO(3)$ (rotation by $\pi$ about the $x_1$-axis), $c'_{ijkl} = (-1)^{n_2 + n_3} c_{ijkl} = (-1)^{4 - n_1} c_{ijkl} = (-1)^{n_1} c_{ijkl}$, where $n_a$ counts occurrences of the index $a$ among $(i,j,k,l)$. Isotropy ($c'_{ijkl} = c_{ijkl}$) forces $c_{ijkl} = 0$ whenever $n_1$ is odd. Similarly, rotating by $\pi$ about the $x_2$- and $x_3$-axes forces $n_2$ and $n_3$ to be even. Thus the only possibly non-zero components have either all four indices equal $(a,a,a,a)$, or two distinct pairs $(a,a,b,b)$, $(a,b,a,b)$, $(a,b,b,a)$ with $a \neq b$.
  2. Proper rotations that permute axes cyclically, e.g. $(x_1,x_2,x_3)\mapsto(x_2,x_3,x_1)$, together with $\pi/2$ rotations such as $(x_1,x_2,x_3)\mapsto(x_1,x_3,-x_2)$ (where any minus sign is raised to an even power and cancels), show that $c_{ijkl}$ is invariant under every permutation of the index values $\{1,2,3\}$. Hence there exist scalars $\kappa, \alpha, \beta, \gamma$ such that
     \[
     c_{aaaa} = \kappa, \quad c_{aabb} = \alpha, \quad c_{abab} = \beta, \quad c_{abba} = \gamma \qquad (a \neq b).
     \]
  3. Finally, apply a rotation by $\pi/4$ about the $x_3$-axis, so $R_{11} = R_{22} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, $R_{12} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, $R_{21} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, $R_{33} = 1$. Computing $c'_{1111} = R_{1p} R_{1q} R_{1r} R_{1s} c_{pqrs}$ over $p,q,r,s \in \{1,2\}$ (noting every non-zero term has an even number of index $2$'s, so all signs are $+1$ and each product of four $R$'s equals $(1/\sqrt{2})^4 = \frac{1}{4}$):
     \[
     \kappa = c'_{1111} = \frac{1}{4}\left[(c_{1111}+c_{2222}) + (c_{1122}+c_{2211}) + (c_{1212}+c_{2121}) + (c_{1221}+c_{2112})\right] = \frac{1}{2}(\kappa + \alpha + \beta + \gamma),
     \]
     which gives $\kappa = \alpha + \beta + \gamma$. Comparing every index pattern shows that
     \[
     c_{ijkl} = \alpha \delta_{ij}\delta_{kl} + \beta \delta_{ik}\delta_{jl} + \gamma \delta_{il}\delta_{jk}.
     \]

Substituting this general isotropic form into $\sigma_{ij} = c_{ijkl} e_{kl}$ yields
\[
\sigma_{ij} = \left(\alpha \delta_{ij}\delta_{kl} + \beta \delta_{ik}\delta_{jl} + \gamma \delta_{il}\delta_{jk}\right) e_{kl} = \alpha e_{kk} \delta_{ij} + \beta e_{ij} + \gamma e_{ji}.
\]
Because the strain tensor $e_{ij}$ is symmetric ($e_{ji} = e_{ij}$), this reduces to
\[
\sigma_{ij} = \alpha e_{kk} \delta_{ij} + (\beta + \gamma) e_{ij}.
\]
Defining the Lamé constants $\lambda = \alpha$ and $\mu = \frac{1}{2}(\beta + \gamma)$, we obtain
\[
\sigma_{ij} = \lambda e_{kk} \delta_{ij} + 2\mu e_{ij}.
\]

### 4. Decomposition $e_{ij} = p\delta_{ij} + d_{ij}$

Suppose $e_{ij} = p\delta_{ij} + d_{ij}$ where $d_{ij}$ is traceless ($d_{ii} = 0$). Contracting $i$ and $j$ (in three dimensions, $\delta_{ii} = 3$) gives
\[
e_{ii} = p \delta_{ii} + d_{ii} = 3p + 0 \implies p = \frac{1}{3} e_{kk}.
\]
Conversely, defining
\[
p = \frac{1}{3} e_{kk}, \qquad d_{ij} = e_{ij} - \frac{1}{3} e_{kk} \delta_{ij},
\]
we verify each required property:
- $p = \frac{1}{3} e_{kk}$ is a scalar, since under $R \in O(3)$, $e'_{kk} = R_{kp} R_{kq} e_{pq} = \delta_{pq} e_{pq} = e_{pp}$.
- $d_{ij} = e_{ij} - p\delta_{ij}$ is a linear combination of two second-rank tensors ($e_{ij}$ and $p\delta_{ij}$), so $d_{ij}$ is a second-rank tensor; moreover it is symmetric ($d_{ji} = e_{ji} - p\delta_{ji} = d_{ij}$) and traceless:
  \[
  d_{ii} = e_{ii} - \frac{1}{3} e_{kk} \delta_{ii} = e_{ii} - \frac{1}{3} e_{kk} \cdot 3 = 0.
  \]
- Evidently $e_{ij} = p\delta_{ij} + d_{ij}$, and the decomposition is unique.

### 5. Necessary and sufficient conditions for $E = \frac{1}{2}\sigma_{ij} e_{ij} \ge 0$

Using $e_{ij} = p\delta_{ij} + d_{ij}$ with $e_{kk} = 3p$ and $d_{ii} = 0$, the stress tensor becomes
\[
\sigma_{ij} = \lambda (3p) \delta_{ij} + 2\mu (p\delta_{ij} + d_{ij}) = (3\lambda + 2\mu) p \delta_{ij} + 2\mu d_{ij}.
\]
Substituting into the stored elastic energy density $E = \frac{1}{2}\sigma_{ij} e_{ij}$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
E &= \frac{1}{2}\left[(3\lambda + 2\mu) p \delta_{ij} + 2\mu d_{ij}\right]\left(p\delta_{ij} + d_{ij}\right) \\
  &= \frac{1}{2}\left[(3\lambda + 2\mu) p^2 \delta_{ij}\delta_{ij} + (3\lambda + 2\mu) p \delta_{ij} d_{ij} + 2\mu p d_{ij}\delta_{ij} + 2\mu d_{ij} d_{ij}\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $\delta_{ij}\delta_{ij} = \delta_{ii} = 3$ and $\delta_{ij} d_{ij} = d_{ii} = 0$, the cross terms vanish identically and we obtain
\[
E = \frac{3}{2}(3\lambda + 2\mu) p^2 + \mu d_{ij} d_{ij}
  = \frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda + \frac{2}{3}\mu\right)(e_{kk})^2 + \mu \sum_{i,j=1}^{3} (d_{ij})^2.
\]
Note that for any real strain tensor $e_{ij}$, both $p^2 \ge 0$ and $d_{ij} d_{ij} = \sum_{i,j=1}^3 d_{ij}^2 \ge 0$.

- **Sufficiency:** Assume $\mu \ge 0$ and $\lambda \ge -\frac{2}{3}\mu$. Then $3\lambda + 2\mu = 3\left(\lambda + \frac{2}{3}\mu\right) \ge 0$ and $\mu \ge 0$. Hence for every deformation (every real symmetric strain tensor $e_{ij}$),
  \[
  E = \frac{3}{2}(3\lambda + 2\mu) p^2 + \mu \sum_{i,j=1}^{3} (d_{ij})^2 \ge 0.
  \]

- **Necessity:** Assume $E \ge 0$ for every deformation of the solid. Recall that any constant real symmetric matrix $e_{ij}$ is achieved by the linear displacement field $u_i(\mathbf{x}) = e_{ij} x_j$ (since $\frac{1}{2}(\partial_i u_j + \partial_j u_i) = e_{ij}$).
  1. Take a **pure shear** deformation, e.g. $e_{12} = e_{21} = 1$ with all other entries $0$ (realised by $\mathbf{u} = (x_2, x_1, 0)$). Then $p = \frac{1}{3} e_{kk} = 0$ and $d_{ij} = e_{ij}$ with $d_{ij} d_{ij} = 2 > 0$. Thus
     \[
     E = 2\mu \ge 0 \implies \mu \ge 0.
     \]
  2. Take a **pure isotropic dilation**, $e_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ (realised by $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{x}$). Then $p = \frac{1}{3}\delta_{kk} = 1$ and $d_{ij} = 0$. Thus
     \[
     E = \frac{3}{2}(3\lambda + 2\mu) \ge 0 \implies 3\lambda + 2\mu \ge 0 \implies \lambda \ge -\frac{2}{3}\mu.
     \]

Therefore $\mu \ge 0$ and $\lambda \ge -\frac{2}{3}\mu$ are necessary and sufficient conditions for $E \ge 0$ for all deformations.

## Final Answers

- **Transformation law:** $T'_{ij\cdots k} = R_{ip} R_{jq} \cdots R_{kr} T_{pq\cdots r}$ ($n$ factors of $R$, with $R$ orthogonal, $R^T R = R R^T = I$).
- **Isotropy:** $c'_{ijkl} = c_{ijkl}$ for all orthogonal $R$, verified via $R_{au} R_{bv}\delta_{uv} = \delta_{ab}$.
- **Stress-strain law:** $\sigma_{ij} = \lambda e_{kk}\delta_{ij} + 2\mu e_{ij}$ with $\lambda = \alpha$ and $\mu = \frac{1}{2}(\beta + \gamma)$.
- **Decomposition of strain:** $e_{ij} = p\delta_{ij} + d_{ij}$ with $p = \frac{1}{3} e_{kk}$ and $d_{ij} = e_{ij} - \frac{1}{3} e_{kk}\delta_{ij}$ (symmetric, traceless).
- **Energy density and positivity conditions:**
  \[
  E = \frac{3}{2}(3\lambda + 2\mu)p^2 + \mu d_{ij} d_{ij} \ge 0 \quad \forall e_{ij} \iff \mu \ge 0 \text{ and } \lambda \ge -\frac{2}{3}\mu.
  \]
