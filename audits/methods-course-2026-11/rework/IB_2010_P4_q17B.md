## Full Solution

### 1. Proof of Green's third identity for harmonic $u$

Let $V \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a bounded regular region with piecewise-smooth boundary surface $S$ and outward unit normal $\mathbf{n}$, let $\mathbf{r}_0 \in V$ be an interior point, and suppose $u \in C^2(V) \cap C^1(\overline{V})$ satisfies Laplace's equation $\nabla^2 u = 0$ in $V$.

**Green's second identity:** For any $\phi, \psi \in C^2(\Omega) \cap C^1(\overline{\Omega})$, applying the Divergence Theorem to $\phi\nabla\psi - \psi\nabla\phi$ gives
$$
\int_\Omega (\phi\nabla^2\psi - \psi\nabla^2\phi)\,dV
= \int_\Omega \nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla\psi - \psi\nabla\phi)\,dV
= \int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\phi\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial n} - \psi\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}\right)dS.
$$

**Excising the singularity at $\mathbf{r}_0$:** Since $G_{f_3}(\mathbf{r}; \mathbf{r}_0) = -\frac{1}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0|}$ is singular at $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_0$, let $B_\varepsilon = \{\mathbf{r} : |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0| < \varepsilon\}$ be a ball with $\overline{B_\varepsilon} \subset V$, boundary sphere $S_\varepsilon = \{\mathbf{r} : |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0| = \varepsilon\}$, and set $V_\varepsilon = V \setminus \overline{B_\varepsilon}$.

Writing $\rho = |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0|$, on $V_\varepsilon$ we have $\rho > 0$ and $G_{f_3} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\rho}$ is smooth and harmonic:
$$
\nabla^2 G_{f_3} = \frac{1}{\rho^2}\frac{d}{d\rho}\!\left(\rho^2\frac{d}{d\rho}\!\left(-\frac{1}{4\pi\rho}\right)\right) = \frac{1}{\rho^2}\frac{d}{d\rho}\!\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\right) = 0 \qquad (\mathbf{r} \in V_\varepsilon).
$$
Since $\nabla^2 u = 0$ on $V_\varepsilon$ as well, Green's second identity applied to $u$ and $G_{f_3}$ on $V_\varepsilon$ gives
$$
0 = \int_{V_\varepsilon}(u\nabla^2 G_{f_3} - G_{f_3}\nabla^2 u)\,dV
= \int_S\left(u\frac{\partial G_{f_3}}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}G_{f_3}\right)dS
+ \int_{S_\varepsilon}\left(u\frac{\partial G_{f_3}}{\partial n_{V_\varepsilon}} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial n_{V_\varepsilon}}G_{f_3}\right)dS,
$$
where $\mathbf{n}_{V_\varepsilon}$ on $S_\varepsilon$ is the outward normal to $V_\varepsilon$, pointing *inward* toward $\mathbf{r}_0$, i.e. $\mathbf{n}_{V_\varepsilon} = -\hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}}$ and $\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{V_\varepsilon}} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}$.

On $S_\varepsilon$ ($\rho = \varepsilon$), we have
$$
G_{f_3} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon}, \qquad
\frac{\partial G_{f_3}}{\partial n_{V_\varepsilon}} = -\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\!\left(-\frac{1}{4\pi\rho}\right)\right|_{\rho=\varepsilon} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon^2}, \qquad
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n_{V_\varepsilon}} = -\frac{\partial u}{\partial\rho}.
$$
Substituting these into the integral over $S_\varepsilon$:
$$
\int_{S_\varepsilon}\left(u\frac{\partial G_{f_3}}{\partial n_{V_\varepsilon}} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial n_{V_\varepsilon}}G_{f_3}\right)dS
= -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon^2}\int_{S_\varepsilon} u(\mathbf{r})\,dS - \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon}\int_{S_\varepsilon}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\rho}\,dS.
$$
Now let $\varepsilon \to 0^+$:
1. By continuity of $u$ at $\mathbf{r}_0$, for any $\delta > 0$ we have $|u(\mathbf{r}) - u(\mathbf{r}_0)| < \delta$ on $S_\varepsilon$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, so
   $$
   \left|\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon^2}\int_{S_\varepsilon} u(\mathbf{r})\,dS - u(\mathbf{r}_0)\right|
   = \left|\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon^2}\int_{S_\varepsilon} (u(\mathbf{r}) - u(\mathbf{r}_0))\,dS\right| < \delta,
   $$
   whence $-\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon^2}\int_{S_\varepsilon} u(\mathbf{r})\,dS \to -u(\mathbf{r}_0)$.
2. Since $u \in C^1$ near $\mathbf{r}_0$, $|\nabla u| \leqslant M$ on some $\overline{B_{\varepsilon_0}}$, so $|\partial u/\partial\rho| \leqslant M$ on $S_\varepsilon$ and
   $$
   \left|\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon}\int_{S_\varepsilon}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\rho}\,dS\right| \leqslant \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon}\cdot 4\pi\varepsilon^2 M = M\varepsilon \to 0.
   $$

Taking the limit $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ therefore yields
$$
0 = \int_S\left(u\frac{\partial G_{f_3}}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}G_{f_3}\right)dS - u(\mathbf{r}_0),
$$
which rearranges to Green's third identity:
$$
u(\mathbf{r}_0) = \int_S\left(u\frac{\partial G_{f_3}}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}G_{f_3}\right)dS.
$$

### 2. Flux condition $\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} p(x, y)\,dx\,dy = 0$ (and a rigorous caveat on the decay exponent $a$)

Consider the hemisphere of radius $R > 0$ in the half-space $z > 0$:
$$
V_R = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : z > 0,\; x^2 + y^2 + z^2 < R^2\}.
$$
Its boundary $\partial V_R = D_R \cup H_R$ consists of:
- the disc $D_R = \{(x, y, 0) : x^2 + y^2 \leqslant R^2\}$ on $z = 0$, where the outward unit normal is $\mathbf{n} = -\hat{\mathbf{z}}$, so $\frac{\partial u}{\partial n} = -\frac{\partial u}{\partial z} = -p(x, y)$;
- the hemispherical surface $H_R = \{\mathbf{x} : |\mathbf{x}| = R,\; z \geqslant 0\}$, where $\mathbf{n} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}$ and $\frac{\partial u}{\partial n} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial r}$.

Applying the Divergence Theorem to $\nabla u$ on $V_R$ and using $\nabla^2 u = 0$:
$$
0 = \int_{V_R}\nabla^2 u\,dV = \int_{\partial V_R}\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\,dS
= -\iint_{x^2+y^2\leqslant R^2} p(x, y)\,dx\,dy + \int_{H_R}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,dS.
$$
Hence, for every $R > 0$,
$$
\iint_{x^2+y^2\leqslant R^2} p(x, y)\,dx\,dy = \int_{H_R}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,dS.
$$
Since the surface area of $H_R$ is $2\pi R^2$ and $\frac{\partial u}{\partial r} = O(R^{-a-1})$ as $R \to \infty$, we have the bound
$$
\left|\int_{H_R}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,dS\right| \leqslant C R^{-a-1}\cdot 2\pi R^2 = O(R^{1-a}).
$$

- **When $a > 1$:** As $R \to \infty$, $R^{1-a} \to 0$, so $\int_{H_R}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,dS \to 0$. Therefore, by conservation of total flux of the harmonic field across $\partial V_R$, the Neumann data must satisfy the solvability condition
  $$
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} p(x, y)\,dx\,dy = 0.
  $$
- **Important mathematical caveat regarding the stated hypothesis $a > 0$:** In three dimensions, the condition $a > 0$ alone is **not** strong enough to force $\int_{H_R}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,dS \to 0$, because $R^2 \cdot R^{-a-1} = R^{1-a}$ does not vanish for $0 < a \leqslant 1$! Indeed, the harmonic function in $z > 0$ given by a point source at $(0, 0, -1)$,
  $$
  u(x, y, z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + (z + 1)^2}},
  $$
  satisfies $\nabla^2 u = 0$ in $z > 0$, $u = O(|\mathbf{x}|^{-1})$, and $\frac{\partial u}{\partial r} = O(|\mathbf{x}|^{-2})$ (so $a = 1 > 0$), yet its boundary normal derivative $p(x, y) = \left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0} = -(x^2 + y^2 + 1)^{-3/2}$ has nonzero total flux:
  $$
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} p(x, y)\,dx\,dy
  = -\int_0^{2\pi}\!d\theta \int_0^{\infty}\!\frac{\rho\,d\rho}{(\rho^2 + 1)^{3/2}} = -2\pi \neq 0.
  $$
  *(By contrast, $a > 0$ IS the exact threshold required in Part 3 for the hemispherical Green's-identity integral $\int_{H_R}(u\frac{\partial G}{\partial r} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial r}G)\,dS = O(R^2 \cdot R^{-a-2}) = O(R^{-a})$ to vanish as $R \to \infty$; and in a 2D half-plane—note the paper's phrase "half plane $z>0$"—the semicircular arc length is $\pi R$, so $a > 0$ would suffice for the flux integral as well. In 3D, $\iint p\,dx\,dy = 0$ is necessary precisely when $a > 1$, i.e. when $u$ decays faster than a $1/r$ monopole.)*

### 3. Construction of the Neumann Green's function $G(\mathbf{r}; \mathbf{r}_0)$ and integral solution

Let $\mathbf{r} = (x, y, z)$ and $\mathbf{r}_0 = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$ with $z_0 > 0$. By the method of images, reflect $\mathbf{r}_0$ across the boundary plane $z = 0$ to obtain the image point $\mathbf{r}_0^* = (x_0, y_0, -z_0)$, which lies outside the domain ($z < 0$).

To achieve $\partial G/\partial z = 0$ at $z = 0$, place an image of the **same sign** at $\mathbf{r}_0^*$:
$$
G(\mathbf{r}; \mathbf{r}_0) = G_{f_3}(\mathbf{r}; \mathbf{r}_0) + G_{f_3}(\mathbf{r}; \mathbf{r}_0^*)
= -\frac{1}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0|} - \frac{1}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0^*|},
$$
explicitly
$$
G(x, y, z; x_0, y_0, z_0)
= -\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 + (z - z_0)^2}}
  -\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 + (z + z_0)^2}}.
$$

Check the properties of $G$:
1. **Harmonic image term:** Since $\mathbf{r}_0^*$ lies in $z < 0$, the function $H(\mathbf{r}; \mathbf{r}_0) := G_{f_3}(\mathbf{r}; \mathbf{r}_0^*)$ is smooth and satisfies $\nabla^2 H = 0$ throughout $z \geqslant 0$. Thus $\nabla^2 G(\mathbf{r}; \mathbf{r}_0) = \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0)$ in $z > 0$.
2. **Neumann boundary condition on $z = 0$:**
   $$
   \frac{\partial G}{\partial z} = \frac{z - z_0}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0|^3} + \frac{z + z_0}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0^*|^3}.
   $$
   At $z = 0$, $|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0| = |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0^*| = \sqrt{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 + z_0^2}$, so
   $$
   \left.\frac{\partial G}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0} = \frac{-z_0 + z_0}{4\pi\big((x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 + z_0^2\big)^{3/2}} = 0.
   $$
3. **Boundary value on $z = 0$:**
   $$
   G(x, y, 0; x_0, y_0, z_0) = -\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 + z_0^2}}.
   $$
4. **Decay at infinity (for fixed $\mathbf{r}_0$):** $G = O(|\mathbf{r}|^{-1})$ and $\frac{\partial G}{\partial r} = O(|\mathbf{r}|^{-2})$ uniformly as $|\mathbf{r}| \to \infty$.

**Deriving the integral representation:** Fix $\mathbf{r}_0$ with $z_0 > 0$ and choose $R > |\mathbf{r}_0|$ so $\mathbf{r}_0 \in V_R$. Since both $u$ and $H$ are harmonic in $V_R$, Green's second identity gives
$$
0 = \int_{\partial V_R}\left(u\frac{\partial H}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}H\right)dS.
$$
Adding this to Green's third identity (Part 1) on $V_R$ yields
$$
u(\mathbf{r}_0) = \int_{\partial V_R}\left(u\frac{\partial G}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}G\right)dS
= \int_{D_R}\left(u\frac{\partial G}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}G\right)dS
+ \int_{H_R}\left(u\frac{\partial G}{\partial r} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial r}G\right)dS.
$$

- On the hemispherical cap $H_R$, since $u = O(R^{-a})$, $\frac{\partial u}{\partial r} = O(R^{-a-1})$, $G = O(R^{-1})$, $\frac{\partial G}{\partial r} = O(R^{-2})$, and $\text{Area}(H_R) = 2\pi R^2$:
  $$
  \left|\int_{H_R}\left(u\frac{\partial G}{\partial r} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial r}G\right)dS\right|
  = O(R^2 \cdot R^{-a-2}) = O(R^{-a}) \to 0 \qquad \text{as } R \to \infty \quad (\text{since } a > 0).
  $$
- On the disc $D_R$ ($z = 0$), the outward normal is $\mathbf{n} = -\hat{\mathbf{z}}$, so $\frac{\partial}{\partial n} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial z}$. Thus $\frac{\partial G}{\partial n} = -\frac{\partial G}{\partial z} = 0$ and $-\frac{\partial u}{\partial n} = +\frac{\partial u}{\partial z} = p(x, y)$. Therefore,
  $$
  \int_{D_R}\left(u\frac{\partial G}{\partial n} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}G\right)dS
  = \iint_{x^2+y^2\leqslant R^2} p(x, y)\,G(x, y, 0; x_0, y_0, z_0)\,dx\,dy.
  $$

Letting $R \to \infty$, we obtain
$$
u(x_0, y_0, z_0) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} p(x, y) f(x - x_0, y - y_0, z_0)\,dx\,dy,
$$
where
$$
f(X, Y, z_0) = -\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{X^2 + Y^2 + z_0^2}},
\qquad \text{i.e.} \qquad
f(x - x_0, y - y_0, z_0) = -\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 + z_0^2}}.
$$

### 4. Asymptotic behaviour as $z_0 \to \infty$

Now let
$$
p(x, y) = \begin{cases} x & |x|, |y| < a, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$
(Note that $\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} p(x, y)\,dx\,dy = \int_{-a}^a\int_{-a}^a x\,dx\,dy = 0$ by oddness in $x$.) The solution is
$$
u(x_0, y_0, z_0) = -\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-a}^{a}\int_{-a}^{a}\frac{x}{\sqrt{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 + z_0^2}}\,dx\,dy.
$$

Fix $(x_0, y_0)$ and let $z_0 \to \infty$. For $z_0 > 0$ sufficiently large that $(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 < z_0^2$ uniformly for all $(x, y) \in [-a, a]^2$, expand $f(x - x_0, y - y_0, z_0)$ by the binomial series $(1 + \eta)^{-1/2} = 1 - \frac{1}{2}\eta + O(\eta^2)$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x - x_0, y - y_0, z_0)
&= -\frac{1}{2\pi z_0}\left(1 + \frac{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2}{z_0^2}\right)^{-1/2} \\
&= -\frac{1}{2\pi z_0}\left[1 - \frac{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2}{2z_0^2} + O(z_0^{-4})\right] \\
&= -\frac{1}{2\pi z_0} + \frac{x^2 - 2x_0 x + x_0^2 + (y - y_0)^2}{4\pi z_0^3} + O(z_0^{-5}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where the $O(z_0^{-5})$ remainder is uniform for $(x, y) \in [-a, a]^2$.

Multiply by $p(x, y) = x$ and integrate over $[-a, a]\times[-a, a]$:
- **Order $z_0^{-1}$ term:**
  $$
  -\frac{1}{2\pi z_0}\int_{-a}^{a}\int_{-a}^{a} x\,dx\,dy = 0 \qquad (\text{odd in } x).
  $$
- **Order $z_0^{-3}$ term:** Since $x^3$, $x_0^2 x$, and $(y - y_0)^2 x$ are all odd functions of $x$, their integrals over $[-a, a]$ vanish. Only the even-in-$x$ term $-2x_0 x^2$ survives:
  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  \frac{1}{4\pi z_0^3}\int_{-a}^{a}\int_{-a}^{a}(-2x_0 x^2)\,dx\,dy
  &= \frac{-2x_0}{4\pi z_0^3}\left(\int_{-a}^{a} x^2\,dx\right)\left(\int_{-a}^{a} dy\right) \\
  &= \frac{-x_0}{2\pi z_0^3}\cdot\left(\frac{2a^3}{3}\right)\cdot(2a)
  = \frac{-2a^4 x_0}{3\pi z_0^3}.
  \end{aligned}
  $$
- **Remainder:** Integrating $x \cdot O(z_0^{-5})$ over the bounded square $[-a, a]^2$ yields $O(z_0^{-5})$.

Hence, as $z_0 \to \infty$ (for fixed $x_0, y_0$),
$$
u(x_0, y_0, z_0) = \frac{-2a^4 x_0}{3\pi z_0^3} + O(z_0^{-5}),
$$
so $u \to \dfrac{-2a^4 x_0}{3\pi z_0^3}$ as $z_0 \to \infty$ (with $u(0, y_0, z_0) \equiv 0$ identically when $x_0 = 0$).

## Final Answers

- **Green's third identity:** Proved by excising a ball $B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}_0)$ and taking $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ in Green's second identity.
- **Necessary flux condition:** $\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} p(x,y)\,dx\,dy = \lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{H_R}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,dS = 0$ when $a > 1$ (since $\int_{H_R}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,dS = O(R^{1-a})$); for $0 < a \leqslant 1$, $u = (x^2 + y^2 + (z+1)^2)^{-1/2}$ ($a=1$) is a counterexample with nonzero flux $-2\pi$.
- **Neumann Green's function for $z > 0$:**
  $$G(\mathbf{r};\mathbf{r}_0) = -\frac{1}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0|} - \frac{1}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_0^*|}, \qquad \mathbf{r}_0^* = (x_0, y_0, -z_0).$$
- **Kernel $f$:**
  $$f(x - x_0, y - y_0, z_0) = -\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 + z_0^2}}.$$
- **Large-$z_0$ asymptotics:**
  $$u(x_0, y_0, z_0) = \frac{-2a^4 x_0}{3\pi z_0^3} + O(z_0^{-5}) \qquad (z_0 \to \infty).$$
