## Full Solution

### Part (a)

**Separation of variables.**
We look for non-trivial separable solutions $\phi(r,\theta) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$ to Laplace's equation in plane polar coordinates:
\[
\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial\theta^2} = 0.
\]
Substituting $\phi(r,\theta) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$ and multiplying by $\dfrac{r^2}{R(r)\Theta(\theta)}$ (away from zeros of $R\Theta$) separates the variables:
\[
\frac{r}{R(r)}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r\frac{dR}{dr}\right) = -\frac{\Theta''(\theta)}{\Theta(\theta)} = \lambda,
\]
where $\lambda$ is a separation constant independent of both $r$ and $\theta$. This yields the pair of ODEs
\[
\Theta''(\theta) + \lambda\Theta(\theta) = 0, \qquad r^2 R''(r) + r R'(r) - \lambda R(r) = 0.
\]
On any full disc, exterior of a disc, or full annulus centred at the origin, the polar angle $\theta$ runs over $[0, 2\pi)$, so single-valuedness and smoothness of $\phi$ require $\Theta$ to be $2\pi$-periodic:
\[
\Theta(\theta + 2\pi) = \Theta(\theta), \qquad \Theta'(\theta + 2\pi) = \Theta'(\theta) \qquad \forall \theta \in \mathbb{R}.
\]
Note that $-\Theta'' = \lambda\Theta$ with periodic boundary conditions on $[0, 2\pi]$ is a self-adjoint Sturm–Liouville problem (boundary term $[\Theta^*\Theta' - (\Theta^*)'\Theta]_0^{2\pi} = 0$), so $\lambda$ must be real (and in fact $\lambda = \int_0^{2\pi}|\Theta'|^2\,d\theta / \int_0^{2\pi}|\Theta|^2\,d\theta \ge 0$). Checking all real values of $\lambda$ explicitly:

1. **$\lambda < 0$:** Put $\lambda = -\mu^2$ with $\mu > 0$. Then $\Theta(\theta) = C_1 e^{\mu\theta} + C_2 e^{-\mu\theta}$. Periodicity $\Theta(0) = \Theta(2\pi)$ and $\Theta'(0) = \Theta'(2\pi)$ gives $C_1(e^{2\pi\mu} - 1) = 0$ and $C_2(e^{-2\pi\mu} - 1) = 0$, hence $C_1 = C_2 = 0$. No non-trivial solution.

2. **$\lambda = 0$:** $\Theta''(\theta) = 0 \implies \Theta(\theta) = A + B\theta$. Periodicity requires $B = 0$, so $\Theta_0(\theta) = \text{const}$.
   The radial equation becomes $\dfrac{d}{dr}\!\left(r\dfrac{dR}{dr}\right) = 0$, so $r R'(r) = c_0$ and hence (for $r > 0$)
   \[
   R_0(r) = a_0 + c_0 \ln r.
   \]

3. **$\lambda > 0$:** Put $\lambda = \mu^2$ with $\mu > 0$. Then $\Theta(\theta) = P\cos(\mu\theta) + Q\sin(\mu\theta)$. Periodicity of period $2\pi$ holds if and only if $\mu = n \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$, so $\lambda = n^2$ with linearly independent angular solutions $\cos(n\theta)$ and $\sin(n\theta)$.
   The radial Euler–Cauchy equation is
   \[
   r^2 R''(r) + r R'(r) - n^2 R(r) = 0.
   \]
   Trying $R(r) = r^\alpha$ gives $\alpha(\alpha - 1) + \alpha - n^2 = \alpha^2 - n^2 = 0$, so $\alpha = \pm n$. Since $n \ge 1$, the roots $n$ and $-n$ are distinct, giving the general radial solution $R_n(r) = \alpha_n r^n + \beta_n r^{-n}$.

By linearity of Laplace's equation, superposing all separable modes ($n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) gives the general real $2\pi$-periodic solution:
\[
\phi(r,\theta) = a_0 + c_0 \ln r + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(a_n r^n + c_n r^{-n}\right)\cos n\theta + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(b_n r^n + d_n r^{-n}\right)\sin n\theta,
\]
for arbitrary real constants $a_i, b_i, c_i, d_i$ (specifically $a_0, c_0$ and $a_n, b_n, c_n, d_n$ for $n \ge 1$).

**Regularity conditions.**
By orthogonality of $\{1, \cos n\theta, \sin n\theta\}$ on $[0, 2\pi]$, each mode's radial coefficient can be extracted by integrating $\phi(r,\theta)$ against the corresponding angular harmonic, so regularity (boundedness and smoothness) of $\phi$ requires every individual singular radial term to have coefficient zero:

- **(i) Interior of a disc centred at the origin ($0 \le r < R$):**
  As $r \to 0^+$, $\ln r \to -\infty$ and $r^{-n} \to +\infty$ for every $n \ge 1$, whereas $1$ and $r^n$ remain bounded (and $r^n\cos n\theta, r^n\sin n\theta$ are harmonic polynomials in $(x, y)$). Hence regularity at $r = 0$ requires
  \[
  c_0 = 0, \qquad c_n = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad d_n = 0 \quad \text{for all } n \ge 1.
  \]
- **(ii) Exterior of a disc centred at the origin ($r > R$, including regularity at $\infty$):**
  As $r \to \infty$, $\ln r \to +\infty$ and $r^n \to +\infty$ for every $n \ge 1$, whereas $a_0$ and $r^{-n}$ remain bounded (under inversion $\rho = 1/r$, $a_0$ and $\rho^n\cos n\theta, \rho^n\sin n\theta$ are regular at $\rho = 0$). Hence regularity in the exterior requires
  \[
  c_0 = 0, \qquad a_n = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad b_n = 0 \quad \text{for all } n \ge 1.
  \]
  (Note that $a_0$ need **not** be zero for regularity/boundedness as $r \to \infty$; only if one additionally imposes decay $\phi \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$ would $a_0 = 0$ be required.)
- **(iii) An annular region centred at the origin ($0 < R_1 < r < R_2 < \infty$):**
  Neither $r = 0$ nor $r = \infty$ belongs to the domain, so $\ln r$, $r^n$, and $r^{-n}$ are all smooth and bounded throughout any compact subset of the annulus. Therefore **none** of the constants must be zero.

### Part (b)

We are given a $2\pi$-periodic function
\[
f(\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n \cos n\theta.
\]

#### (i) General solution in the annulus $1 < r < e^2$

Since $1 < r < e^2$ is an annulus centred at the origin, by part (a)(iii) we retain all terms of the general solution:
\[
\phi(r,\theta) = a_0 + c_0\ln r + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(a_n r^n + c_n r^{-n}\right)\cos n\theta + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(b_n r^n + d_n r^{-n}\right)\sin n\theta.
\]
Evaluating at the two boundaries $r = 1$ (where $\ln 1 = 0$, $1^{\pm n} = 1$) and $r = e^2$ (where $\ln(e^2) = 2$, $(e^2)^{\pm n} = e^{\pm 2n}$):
\[
\phi(1,\theta) = a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty (a_n + c_n)\cos n\theta + \sum_{n=1}^\infty (b_n + d_n)\sin n\theta = -1 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n\cos n\theta,
\]
\[
\phi(e^2,\theta) = (a_0 + 2c_0) + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(a_n e^{2n} + c_n e^{-2n}\right)\cos n\theta + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(b_n e^{2n} + d_n e^{-2n}\right)\sin n\theta = 1 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n\cos n\theta.
\]
Equating Fourier coefficients by orthogonality of $\{1, \cos n\theta, \sin n\theta\}$ on $[0, 2\pi]$:

1. **$n = 0$ mode:**
   \[
   a_0 = -1, \qquad a_0 + 2c_0 = 1 \implies 2c_0 = 2 \implies c_0 = 1.
   \]
2. **$\sin(n\theta)$ modes ($n \ge 1$):**
   \[
   b_n + d_n = 0, \qquad b_n e^{2n} + d_n e^{-2n} = 0.
   \]
   The determinant is $e^{-2n} - e^{2n} = -2\sinh(2n) \ne 0$ for $n \ge 1$, so $b_n = d_n = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$.
3. **$\cos(n\theta)$ modes ($n \ge 1$):**
   \[
   a_n + c_n = A_n, \qquad a_n e^{2n} + c_n e^{-2n} = A_n.
   \]
   Substituting $c_n = A_n - a_n$ into the second equation gives
   \[
   a_n\left(e^{2n} - e^{-2n}\right) = A_n\left(1 - e^{-2n}\right).
   \]
   Factoring $e^{2n} - e^{-2n} = \left(e^{2n} + 1\right)\left(1 - e^{-2n}\right)$ (and noting $1 - e^{-2n} \ne 0$ for $n \ge 1$) yields
   \[
   a_n = \frac{A_n}{e^{2n} + 1}, \qquad c_n = A_n - a_n = \frac{e^{2n} A_n}{e^{2n} + 1}.
   \]

Therefore the radial factor for mode $n \ge 1$ is
\[
a_n r^n + c_n r^{-n} = \frac{A_n}{e^{2n} + 1}\left(r^n + e^{2n} r^{-n}\right) = A_n\,\frac{e^{n(\ln r - 1)} + e^{-n(\ln r - 1)}}{e^n + e^{-n}} = A_n\,\frac{\cosh\bigl(n(\ln r - 1)\bigr)}{\cosh n}.
\]
Thus the solution for general $f(\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n\cos n\theta$ is
\[
\phi(r,\theta) = \ln r - 1 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{A_n}{e^{2n} + 1}\left(r^n + e^{2n} r^{-n}\right)\cos n\theta
= \ln r - 1 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n\,\frac{\cosh\bigl(n(\ln r - 1)\bigr)}{\cosh n}\cos n\theta.
\]

#### (ii) Explicit solution for the specific $f(\theta)$

We are given the $2\pi$-periodic function
\[
f(\theta) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta, & 0 \le \theta < \pi, \\[6pt] -\dfrac{3\pi}{2} + \theta, & \pi \le \theta < 2\pi. \end{cases}
\]
First verify that $f$ indeed has the pure cosine form $\sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n\cos n\theta$:
- **Evenness (under $2\pi$-periodic extension):** For $\theta \in (0, \pi)$, $2\pi - \theta \in (\pi, 2\pi)$, so
  \[
  f(2\pi - \theta) = -\frac{3\pi}{2} + (2\pi - \theta) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \theta = f(\theta),
  \]
  and $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ (matching $-\frac{\pi}{2}$ at $\theta = \pi$ and $+\frac{\pi}{2}$ at $\theta = 0, 2\pi$). Thus $f(-\theta) = f(2\pi - \theta) = f(\theta)$, so all sine coefficients vanish:
  \[
  B_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\sin n\theta\,d\theta = 0 \qquad (n \ge 1).
  \]
- **Zero mean:**
  \[
  \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\theta = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right)d\theta = \frac{1}{\pi}\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\theta - \frac{\theta^2}{2}\right]_0^\pi = 0.
  \]
- **Cosine coefficients ($n \ge 1$):** Using evenness $f(2\pi - \theta) = f(\theta)$ and $\cos\bigl(n(2\pi - \theta)\bigr) = \cos n\theta$,
  \[
  A_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\cos n\theta\,d\theta = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right)\cos n\theta\,d\theta.
  \]
  Integrating by parts:
  \[
  \int_0^\pi \left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right)\cos n\theta\,d\theta
  = \left[\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right)\frac{\sin n\theta}{n}\right]_0^\pi + \frac{1}{n}\int_0^\pi \sin n\theta\,d\theta
  = 0 + \frac{1}{n}\left[-\frac{\cos n\theta}{n}\right]_0^\pi
  = \frac{1 - (-1)^n}{n^2}.
  \]
  Hence
  \[
  A_n = \frac{2\bigl(1 - (-1)^n\bigr)}{\pi n^2} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{4}{\pi n^2}, & n \text{ odd}, \\[6pt] 0, & n \text{ even}. \end{cases}
  \]
  (Since $f$ is continuous and piecewise smooth on $\mathbb{R}$, its Fourier series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{4}{\pi(2k+1)^2}\cos\bigl((2k+1)\theta\bigr)$ converges uniformly to $f(\theta)$ everywhere.)

Substituting $A_{2k+1} = \dfrac{4}{\pi(2k+1)^2}$ ($k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) and $A_{2k} = 0$ into part (b)(i) yields the explicit solution:
\[
\phi(r,\theta) = \ln r - 1 + \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{r^{2k+1} + e^{2(2k+1)} r^{-(2k+1)}}{(2k+1)^2\left(e^{2(2k+1)} + 1\right)}\cos\bigl((2k+1)\theta\bigr),
\]
or equivalently
\[
\phi(r,\theta) = \ln r - 1 + \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{\cosh\bigl((2k+1)(\ln r - 1)\bigr)}{(2k+1)^2\cosh(2k+1)}\cos\bigl((2k+1)\theta\bigr).
\]

## Final Answers

- **(a)** General solution derived by separation of variables as stated. Regularity constraints:
  - **(i) Interior of disc:** $c_0 = 0$ and $c_n = d_n = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$.
  - **(ii) Exterior of disc:** $c_0 = 0$ and $a_n = b_n = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$ ($a_0$ unrestricted for boundedness/regularity at $\infty$).
  - **(iii) Annular region:** none of the constants must be zero.
- **(b)(i)**
  \[
  \phi(r,\theta) = \ln r - 1 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{A_n}{e^{2n} + 1}\left(r^n + e^{2n} r^{-n}\right)\cos n\theta
  = \ln r - 1 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n\,\frac{\cosh\bigl(n(\ln r - 1)\bigr)}{\cosh n}\cos n\theta.
  \]
- **(b)(ii)** With $A_n = \frac{4}{\pi n^2}$ for odd $n$ and $0$ for even $n$:
  \[
  \phi(r,\theta) = \ln r - 1 + \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{r^{2k+1} + e^{2(2k+1)} r^{-(2k+1)}}{(2k+1)^2\left(e^{2(2k+1)} + 1\right)}\cos\bigl((2k+1)\theta\bigr)
