## Full Solution

### Part (a)

On $[-\pi, \pi]$, we have $f(x) = |x|$. Extended $2\pi$-periodically to $\mathbb{R}$, $f$ is continuous everywhere (since $f(-\pi) = \pi = f(\pi)$ and $f(0^-) = 0 = f(0^+)$), piecewise $C^1$, and **even** ($f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x$). Consequently, its Fourier series converges pointwise (in fact uniformly) to $f(x)$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

#### Sine coefficients $b_n$ ($n \ge 1$)

Since $f(x)$ is even and $\sin(nx)$ is odd, $f(x)\sin(nx)$ is odd on $[-\pi, \pi]$, so
\[
b_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx = 0 \qquad \text{for all } n \ge 1.
\]

#### Constant term $a_0$

With the given convention $f(x) = \frac{1}{2}a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n\cos(nx) + \sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin(nx)$,
\[
a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\,dx = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi} x\,dx = \frac{2}{\pi}\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{\pi} = \pi,
\]
so $\frac{1}{2}a_0 = \frac{\pi}{2}$.

#### Cosine coefficients $a_n$ ($n \ge 1$)

Since $f(x)\cos(nx)$ is even, integrating by parts gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_n &= \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\cos(nx)\,dx = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi} x\cos(nx)\,dx \\
&= \frac{2}{\pi}\left(\left[\frac{x\sin(nx)}{n}\right]_{0}^{\pi} - \frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{\pi} \sin(nx)\,dx\right) \\
&= \frac{2}{\pi}\left(0 + \left[\frac{\cos(nx)}{n^2}\right]_{0}^{\pi}\right) \\
&= \frac{2}{\pi n^2}\big((-1)^n - 1\big).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus:
\[
a_n = \begin{cases}
0, & n \text{ even } (n \ge 2), \\[4pt]
-\dfrac{4}{\pi n^2}, & n \text{ odd } (n \ge 1).
\end{cases}
\]

Hence the Fourier series of $f(x)$ is
\[
f(x) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{\substack{n=1 \\ n\text{ odd}}}^{\infty} \frac{\cos(nx)}{n^2}
= \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{\cos((2m+1)x)}{(2m+1)^2}.
\]

### Part (b)

We seek the general $2\pi$-periodic solution to
\[
y'' + 2y' + y = f(x).
\]

#### Step 1: Homogeneous (complementary) solution and uniqueness

Consider first the homogeneous equation $y_h'' + 2y_h' + y_h = 0$. Its characteristic equation is
\[
r^2 + 2r + 1 = (r + 1)^2 = 0,
\]
which has a repeated root $r = -1$. Therefore the general solution of the homogeneous ODE is
\[
y_h(x) = (C_1 + C_2 x)\,e^{-x},
\]
where $C_1, C_2$ are arbitrary constants.

We now check whether any non-trivial $y_h$ is $2\pi$-periodic. Requiring $y_h(x + 2\pi) = y_h(x)$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ gives
\[
(C_1 + C_2 x + 2\pi C_2)\,e^{-2\pi} e^{-x} = (C_1 + C_2 x)\,e^{-x} \qquad \forall x \in \mathbb{R}.
\]
Multiplying by $e^x$ yields
\[
C_2 e^{-2\pi} x + (C_1 + 2\pi C_2)e^{-2\pi} = C_2 x + C_1 \qquad \forall x \in \mathbb{R}.
\]
Comparing coefficients of $x$ gives $C_2(1 - e^{-2\pi}) = 0 \implies C_2 = 0$ (as $e^{-2\pi} \neq 1$), and then comparing constant terms gives $C_1(1 - e^{-2\pi}) = 0 \implies C_1 = 0$. (Equivalently, if $(C_1, C_2) \neq (0,0)$, then $|y_h(x)| \to \infty$ as $x \to -\infty$, contradicting the boundedness of a continuous periodic function.)

Hence the **only** $2\pi$-periodic homogeneous solution is $y_h(x) \equiv 0$, so the $2\pi$-periodic solution of $y'' + 2y' + y = f(x)$ is **unique** (no free constants remain).

#### Step 2: Finding the $2\pi$-periodic solution by Fourier series

Since $f$ is continuous and $2\pi$-periodic, any $2\pi$-periodic solution $y$ of $y'' + 2y' + y = f(x)$ is $C^2$ and $2\pi$-periodic, so $y, y', y''$ admit Fourier expansions related by term-by-term differentiation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
y(x) &= \frac{1}{2}A_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \big(A_n\cos(nx) + B_n\sin(nx)\big), \\
y'(x) &= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \big(-nA_n\sin(nx) + nB_n\cos(nx)\big), \\
y''(x) &= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \big(-n^2 A_n\cos(nx) - n^2 B_n\sin(nx)\big).
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting into $y'' + 2y' + y = f(x)$ gives
\[
\frac{1}{2}A_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big[\big((1 - n^2)A_n + 2n B_n\big)\cos(nx) + \big(-2n A_n + (1 - n^2)B_n\big)\sin(nx)\Big]
= \frac{1}{2}a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\cos(nx).
\]
Equating Fourier coefficients by orthogonality of $\{1, \cos(nx), \sin(nx)\}$ on $[-\pi, \pi]$:

- **For $n = 0$**:
  \[
  \frac{1}{2}A_0 = \frac{1}{2}a_0 = \frac{\pi}{2} \implies A_0 = \pi.
  \]
- **For $n \ge 1$**:
  \[
  \begin{pmatrix} 1 - n^2 & 2n \\ -2n & 1 - n^2 \end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix} A_n \\ B_n \end{pmatrix}
  = \begin{pmatrix} a_n \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  The determinant is
  \[
  (1 - n^2)^2 + (2n)^2 = 1 - 2n^2 + n^4 + 4n^2 = (n^2 + 1)^2 \neq 0 \qquad \forall n \ge 1.
  \]
  Inverting the $2 \times 2$ matrix yields
  \[
  \begin{pmatrix} A_n \\ B_n \end{pmatrix}
  = \frac{1}{(n^2 + 1)^2}
  \begin{pmatrix} 1 - n^2 & -2n \\ 2n & 1 - n^2 \end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix} a_n \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}
  = \frac{a_n}{(n^2 + 1)^2}
  \begin{pmatrix} 1 - n^2 \\ 2n \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  (Notice that in complex form, dividing $a_n e^{inx}$ by $(in)^2 + 2(in) + 1 = (1 + in)^2$ and taking the real part gives $\operatorname{Re}\!\left[\frac{(1-n^2-2in)(\cos nx + i\sin nx)}{(n^2+1)^2}\right]a_n = \frac{(1-n^2)\cos(nx) + 2n\sin(nx)}{(n^2+1)^2}a_n$, confirming both signs.)

Substituting $a_n = 0$ for even $n \ge 2$ and $a_n = -\frac{4}{\pi n^2}$ for odd $n \ge 1$:
- For **even $n \ge 2$**: $A_n = B_n = 0$.
- For **odd $n \ge 1$**:
  \[
  A_n = -\frac{4(1 - n^2)}{\pi n^2 (n^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{4(n^2 - 1)}{\pi n^2 (n^2 + 1)^2},
  \qquad
  B_n = -\frac{8}{\pi n (n^2 + 1)^2}.
  \]
(Note that for $n = 1$, $A_1 = 0$ and $B_1 = -\frac{2}{\pi}$.)

Therefore, the general (and unique) $2\pi$-periodic solution is
\[
y(x) = \frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{\substack{n=1 \\ n\text{ odd}}}^{\infty} \frac{(n^2 - 1)\cos(nx) - 2n\sin(nx)}{n^2(n^2 + 1)^2},
\]
or equivalently with $n = 2m + 1$ ($m \ge 0$):
\[
y(x) = \frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{\big((2m+1)^2 - 1\big)\cos((2m+1)x) - 2(2m+1)\sin((2m+1)x)}{(2m+1)^2\big((2m+1)^2 + 1\big)^2}.
\]

#### Justification of term-by-term differentiation

The coefficients satisfy $|A_n| = O(n^{-4})$ and $|B_n| = O(n^{-5})$, so the Fourier series for $y$, $y'$ (terms $O(n^{-3})$), and $y''$ (terms $O(n^{-2})$) all converge absolutely and uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$ by the Weierstrass $M$-test. Hence $y \in C^2(\mathbb{R})$ is indeed a classical $2\pi$-periodic solution of $y'' + 2y' + y = f(x)$.

*(Cross-check: Solving $y'' + 2y' + y = \pm x$ directly on $[0,\pi]$ and $[-\pi,0]$ and matching $C^1$ continuity at $x = 0$ and $2\pi$-periodicity at $x = \pm\pi$ yields the unique closed-form expression on $[-\pi,\pi]$:*
\[
y(x) = \begin{cases}
x - 2 + (c_1 + c_2 x)e^{-x}, & x \in [0, \pi], \\
-x + 2 + (d_1 + d_2 x)e^{-x}, & x \in [-\pi, 0],
\end{cases}
\]
*with $d_2 = -\frac{2}{e^\pi+1}$, $c_2 = \frac{2e^\pi}{e^\pi+1}$, $d_1 = -\frac{4}{e^\pi+1} - \frac{2\pi e^\pi}{(e^\pi+1)^2}$, $c_1 = d_1 + 4$, which agrees numerically with the Fourier series to machine/truncation accuracy.)*
