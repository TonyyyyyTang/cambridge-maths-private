## Full Solution

Throughout we assume the diffusivity $D > 0$.

### (a) One-dimensional diffusion Green's function via Fourier transform

We seek the causal Green's function $G(t, t', x, x')$ satisfying
\[
\frac{\partial G}{\partial t} - D\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial x^2} = \delta(t-t')\,\delta(x-x'),
\]
subject to causality $G(t, t', x, x') = 0$ for $t < t'$ and spatial decay $G, \frac{\partial G}{\partial x} \to 0$ as $|x| \to \infty$.

Apply the spatial Fourier transform with respect to $x$,
\[
\tilde{G}(t, t', k, x') = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} G(t, t', x, x') e^{-ikx}\,dx.
\]
Integrating by parts twice and using $G, \partial_x G \to 0$ as $|x|\to\infty$,
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial x^2} e^{-ikx}\,dx = \left[\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}e^{-ikx}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty} + ik\left[G e^{-ikx}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty} + (ik)^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} G e^{-ikx}\,dx = -k^2 \tilde{G}.
\]
For the right-hand side, the sifting property of the Dirac delta gives
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta(t-t')\,\delta(x-x') e^{-ikx}\,dx = \delta(t-t')\,e^{-ikx'}.
\]
Thus $\tilde{G}(t, t', k, x')$ satisfies the first-order linear ODE in $t$:
\[
\frac{\partial \tilde{G}}{\partial t} + Dk^2 \tilde{G} = \delta(t-t')\,e^{-ikx'},
\]
with causality condition $\tilde{G}(t, t', k, x') = 0$ for $t < t'$.

Multiplying by the integrating factor $e^{Dk^2 t}$ gives
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Bigl(e^{Dk^2 t}\tilde{G}(t, t', k, x')\Bigr) = e^{Dk^2 t}\delta(t-t')\,e^{-ikx'} = e^{Dk^2 t'} e^{-ikx'}\,\delta(t-t').
\]
Integrating from $-\infty$ to $t$ and using $\tilde{G}(t, t', k, x') = 0$ for $t < t'$:
\[
e^{Dk^2 t}\tilde{G}(t, t', k, x') = e^{Dk^2 t'} e^{-ikx'} \int_{-\infty}^{t} \delta(\tau - t')\,d\tau = e^{Dk^2 t'} e^{-ikx'} H(t-t'),
\]
where $H$ is the Heaviside step function ($H(s) = 0$ for $s < 0$, $H(s) = 1$ for $s > 0$). Dividing by $e^{Dk^2 t}$ yields
\[
\tilde{G}(t, t', k, x') = H(t-t')\,e^{-ikx'} e^{-Dk^2(t-t')},
\]
as required. (Equivalently, solving the homogeneous ODE for $t \neq t'$ gives $\tilde{G} = 0$ for $t < t'$ and $\tilde{G} = A(k,x') e^{-Dk^2(t-t')}$ for $t > t'$, while integrating the ODE across $(t'-\epsilon, t'+\epsilon)$ gives the jump $\tilde{G}(t'^+) - \tilde{G}(t'^-) = e^{-ikx'}$, hence $A = e^{-ikx'}$.)

To recover $G(t, t', x, x')$, apply the inverse Fourier transform:
\[
G(t, t', x, x') = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{G}(t, t', k, x') e^{ikx}\,dk = \frac{H(t-t')}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-Dk^2(t-t')} e^{ik(x-x')}\,dk.
\]
For $t < t'$, $H(t-t') = 0$ so $G = 0$. For $t > t'$, set $a = D(t-t') > 0$ and $z = x - x'$. By the given hint, the Gaussian $F(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi a}} e^{-\frac{z^2}{4a}}$ has Fourier transform $\tilde{F}(k) = e^{-ak^2}$. Applying the inverse Fourier transform formula to $\tilde{F}$ gives
\[
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ak^2} e^{ikz}\,dk = F(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi a}} e^{-\frac{z^2}{4a}}.
\]
Substituting $a = D(t-t')$ and $z = x - x'$ yields
\[
G(t, t', x, x') = \frac{H(t-t')}{\sqrt{4\pi D(t-t')}} e^{-\frac{(x-x')^2}{4D(t-t')}},
\]
as required.

### (b) Two-dimensional diffusion with a point source

The 2D causal Green's function
\[
G(t, t', x, x', y, y') = \frac{H(t-t')}{4\pi D(t-t')} e^{-\frac{1}{4D(t-t')}\left[(x-x')^2 + (y-y')^2\right]}
\]
satisfies
\[
\frac{\partial G}{\partial t} - D\left(\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial y^2}\right) = \delta(t-t')\,\delta(x-x')\,\delta(y-y'),
\]
with $G = 0$ for $t < t'$.

#### General solution for $t \ge 0$ with $\Psi(0, x, y) = 0$

By linearity (superposition), a solution for $t \ge 0$ to
\[
\frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t} - D\left(\frac{\partial^2 \Psi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \Psi}{\partial y^2}\right) = p(t)\,\delta(x)\,\delta(y)
\]
satisfying the homogeneous initial condition $\Psi(0, x, y) = 0$ (and decaying as $x^2 + y^2 \to \infty$) is constructed by integrating the Green's function against the source over $t' \in [0, \infty)$ and $(x', y') \in \mathbb{R}^2$:
\[
\Psi(t, x, y) = \int_0^{\infty} dt' \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dx' \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dy'\, G(t, t', x, x', y, y')\, p(t')\,\delta(x')\,\delta(y').
\]
Evaluating the spatial delta functions sets $x' = 0$, $y' = 0$, and the Heaviside factor $H(t-t')$ restricts the time integral to $0 \le t' \le t$:
\[
\Psi(t, x, y) = \int_0^t \frac{p(t')}{4\pi D(t-t')} e^{-\frac{x^2+y^2}{4D(t-t')}}\,dt'.
\]
Let us verify that this satisfies both the PDE and the initial condition:
- Applying $\partial_t - D\nabla^2$ under the integral sign gives $\int_0^\infty dt'\,\delta(t-t') p(t')\delta(x)\delta(y) = p(t)\delta(x)\delta(y)$ for $t > 0$.
- As $t \to 0^+$, for any $(x,y) \neq (0,0)$ the integrand vanishes exponentially fast (and in $L^1(\mathbb{R}^2)$ the integral of the heat kernel is $1$, so $\|\Psi(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^1} \le \int_0^t |p(t')|\,dt' \to 0$), confirming $\Psi(0, x, y) = 0$.

#### Exact solution for $t > t_0$ with piecewise-constant $p(t)$

Now set
\[
p(t) = \begin{cases} p_0 = \mathrm{const} & \text{for } 0 \le t \le t_0, \\ 0 & \text{for } t > t_0. \end{cases}
\]
For $t > t_0$, the integrand vanishes for $t' > t_0$, so the upper limit becomes $t_0$:
\[
\Psi(t, x, y) = \frac{p_0}{4\pi D} \int_0^{t_0} \frac{1}{t-t'} e^{-\frac{x^2+y^2}{4D(t-t')}}\,dt'.
\]
Let $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$. For $(x,y) \neq (0,0)$ (so $r^2 > 0$), make the substitution
\[
\lambda = \frac{r^2}{4D(t-t')}.
\]
Differentiating with respect to $t'$ gives
\[
\frac{d\lambda}{dt'} = \frac{r^2}{4D(t-t')^2} = \frac{\lambda}{t-t'} \implies \frac{dt'}{t-t'} = \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.
\]
As $t'$ runs from $0$ to $t_0$ (with $t > t_0$), $t - t'$ decreases from $t$ to $t - t_0 > 0$, so $\lambda$ increases from
\[
\eta_1 = \frac{x^2+y^2}{4Dt} \qquad \text{to} \qquad \eta_2 = \frac{x^2+y^2}{4D(t-t_0)},
\]
with $0 < \eta_1 < \eta_2$. Therefore,
\[
\Psi(t, x, y) = \frac{p_0}{4\pi D} \int_{\eta_1}^{\eta_2} \frac{e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda}\,d\lambda = \frac{p_0}{4\pi D}\left(\int_{\eta_1}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda}\,d\lambda - \int_{\eta_2}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda}\,d\lambda\right).
\]
Using the given definition $Ei(-\eta) = -\int_{\eta}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda}\,d\lambda$, we have $\int_\eta^\infty \frac{e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda}\,d\lambda = -Ei(-\eta)$, and hence for $t > t_0$:
\[
\Psi(t, x, y) = \frac{p_0}{4\pi D}\left[ Ei\!\left(-\frac{x^2+y^2}{4D(t-t_0)}\right) - Ei\!\left(-\frac{x^2+y^2}{4Dt}\right) \right].
\]
(Note on sign: since $0 < \eta_1 < \eta_2$ and $Ei(-\eta)$ is strictly increasing in $\eta > 0$, we have $Ei(-\eta_2) > Ei(-\eta_1)$, so $\Psi(t,x,y) > 0$ for $p_0 > 0$, as physically required.)

#### Small-$\eta$ simplification

The approximation $Ei(-\eta) \approx \ln\eta + C$ applies to both terms when both arguments are small. Since $\eta_1 < \eta_2$, this requires
\[
\eta_2 = \frac{x^2+y^2}{4D(t-t_0)} \ll 1
\]
(which automatically implies $\eta_1 = \frac{x^2+y^2}{4Dt} \ll 1$; physically, this holds near the origin or at late times $t - t_0 \gg \frac{x^2+y^2}{4D}$). Substituting $Ei(-\eta) \approx \ln\eta + C$ into both terms:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Psi(t, x, y) &\approx \frac{p_0}{4\pi D}\left[\left(\ln\!\left(\frac{x^2+y^2}{4D(t-t_0)}\right) + C\right) - \left(\ln\!\left(\frac{x^2+y^2}{4Dt}\right) + C\right)\right] \\
&= \frac{p_0}{4\pi D}\ln\!\left(\frac{\frac{x^2+y^2}{4D(t-t_0)}}{\frac{x^2+y^2}{4Dt}}\right) \\
&= \frac{p_0}{4\pi D}\ln\!\left(\frac{t}{t-t_0}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Observe that Euler's constant $C$, $x^2+y^2$, and $4D$ inside the logarithm all cancel! In fact, at the origin $x = y = 0$ (for $t > t_0$), the direct integral evaluates identically to $\frac{p_0}{4\pi D}\int_0^{t_0}\frac{dt'}{t-t'} = \frac{p_0}{4\pi D}\ln\left(\frac{t}{t-t_0}\right)$. (Furthermore, if also $t \gg t_0$, then $\ln\frac{t}{t-t_0} = -\ln(1 - t_0/t) \approx \frac{t_0}{t}$, giving $\Psi \approx \frac{p_0 t_0}{4\pi D t}$, matching a 2D impulsive point source of total strength $p_0 t_0$.)
