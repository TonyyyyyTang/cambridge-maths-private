## Full Solution

We are asked to solve the first-order linear homogeneous PDE
\[
-y\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + x\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = 0, \qquad (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2,
\]
with Cauchy data on the non-negative $y$-axis $\Gamma = \{(0, s) : s \ge 0\}$ given by
\[
u(0, s) = \cos(s^2) \qquad \text{for } s \ge 0.
\]

### Characteristic equations

Parametrise each characteristic curve by a parameter $t \in \mathbb{R}$, starting at $t = 0$ from the point $(0, s) \in \Gamma$ (with $s \ge 0$). The characteristic equations are
\[
\frac{dx}{dt} = -y, \qquad \frac{dy}{dt} = x, \qquad \frac{du}{dt} = 0,
\]
subject to the initial conditions at $t = 0$:
\[
x(0, s) = 0, \qquad y(0, s) = s \quad (s \ge 0), \qquad u(0, s) = \cos(s^2).
\]

### Solving for the characteristic curves

Differentiating $\frac{dx}{dt} = -y$ with respect to $t$ gives
\[
\frac{d^2 x}{dt^2} = -\frac{dy}{dt} = -x,
\]
whose general solution is
\[
x(t, s) = A(s)\cos t + B(s)\sin t, \qquad y(t, s) = -\frac{dx}{dt} = A(s)\sin t - B(s)\cos t.
\]
Imposing the initial conditions at $t = 0$:
\[
x(0, s) = A(s) = 0, \qquad y(0, s) = -B(s) = s \implies B(s) = -s.
\]
Hence the characteristic curves are
\[
x(t, s) = -s\sin t, \qquad y(t, s) = s\cos t.
\]
(Equivalently, directly from the characteristic ODEs we see $\frac{d}{dt}(x^2 + y^2) = 2x(-y) + 2y(x) = 0$, so $x^2 + y^2 = \text{const} = s^2$ along every characteristic.)

Geometrically:
- For each $s > 0$, as $t$ runs over $[0, 2\pi)$ the characteristic curve is the circle $x^2 + y^2 = s^2$ of radius $s$ centred at the origin, traversed anticlockwise starting from $(0, s)$.
- For $s = 0$, the characteristic degenerates to the single fixed point $(0, 0)$.

### Transversality, coverage of $\mathbb{R}^2$, and inversion

Check the Jacobian of $(t, s) \mapsto (x(t, s), y(t, s))$:
\[
J(t, s) = \frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(t, s)} = \det\begin{pmatrix} -s\cos t & -\sin t \\ -s\sin t & \cos t \end{pmatrix} = -s(\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t) = -s.
\]
For every $s > 0$, $J(t, s) = -s \neq 0$ (in particular on $\Gamma \setminus \{(0,0)\}$, the characteristic vector $(-y, x) = (-s, 0)$ is orthogonal and hence transverse to $\Gamma$). Moreover:
- Every point $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ lies on a unique circle $x^2 + y^2 = s^2$ with $s = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} > 0$, which intersects the data half-line $\Gamma = \{(0, s) : s \ge 0\}$ at **precisely one point** $(0, s)$.
- Because $u$ is constant along each closed characteristic circle, there is no multi-valuedness as $t$ increases by $2\pi$.

### Solution for $u(x, y)$

Integrating $\frac{du}{dt} = 0$ with $u(0, s) = \cos(s^2)$ gives
\[
u(t, s) = \cos(s^2).
\]
Since $x^2 + y^2 = (-s\sin t)^2 + (s\cos t)^2 = s^2$ for all $s \ge 0$ and $t \in \mathbb{R}$ (including $(x, y) = (0, 0)$ where $s = 0$ and $u(0,0) = \cos 0 = 1$), we obtain
\[
u(x, y) = \cos(x^2 + y^2) \qquad \text{for all } (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2.
\]

### Verification and uniqueness

1. **PDE and smoothness:** $u(x, y) = \cos(x^2 + y^2)$ is $C^\infty$ on all of $\mathbb{R}^2$ (including at the origin $(0,0)$, since $\cos(r^2)$ is a smooth function of $r^2 = x^2+y^2$). Its partial derivatives are
   \[
   \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = -2x\sin(x^2 + y^2), \qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = -2y\sin(x^2 + y^2),
   \]
   so
   \[
   -y\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + x\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = 2xy\sin(x^2+y^2) - 2xy\sin(x^2+y^2) = 0 \qquad \forall (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2.
   \]
2. **Boundary condition:** On $x = 0$, $y \ge 0$, $u(0, y) = \cos(0^2 + y^2) = \cos(y^2)$.
3. **Uniqueness:** Any $C^1$ solution on $\mathbb{R}^2$ must be constant along every characteristic circle $\gamma_s(t) = (-s\sin t, s\cos t)$ ($s > 0$), forcing $u(x, y) = u(0, \sqrt{x^2+y^2}) = \cos(x^2+y^2)$ on $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\}$, and $u(0,0) = \cos(0) = 1$ at the origin. Thus $u(x, y) = \cos(x^2 + y^2)$ is the unique solution on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

(Geometrically, in polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ the PDE is simply $\frac{\partial u}{\partial \theta} = 0$, confirming that solutions are radially symmetric functions $u = F(r)$.)

- Unique solution on $\mathbb{R}^2$:
  \[
  u(x, y) = \cos(x^2 + y^2).
