## Full Solution

### 1. Sturm–Liouville eigenvalue form

Observe that by the product rule,
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\!\left((1 - x^2)\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = (1 - x^2)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 2x\frac{dy}{dx}.
\]
The Legendre equation
\[
(1 - x^2)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 2x\frac{dy}{dx} + n(n+1)y = 0, \qquad -1 \le x \le 1,
\]
is therefore already in Sturm–Liouville form. Defining the linear differential operator $L$ by
\[
L y := -\frac{d}{dx}\!\left((1 - x^2)\frac{dy}{dx}\right),
\]
the Legendre equation becomes the Sturm–Liouville eigenvalue equation
\[
L y = \lambda y, \qquad \lambda = \lambda_n = n(n+1) \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, \dots).
\]
Comparing with the general Sturm–Liouville form $L y = -\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(p(x)\frac{dy}{dx}\right) + q(x)y = \lambda w(x) y$ on $[a,b] = [-1,1]$, we identify
\[
p(x) = 1 - x^2, \qquad q(x) = 0, \qquad w(x) = 1.
\]
*(Remark on sign convention: one may equivalently define $\tilde{L}y := \frac{d}{dx}\!\left((1-x^2)\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = -Ly$, yielding the eigenvalue equation $\tilde{L}y = \mu y$ with $\mu = -n(n+1)$, or $\tilde{L}y + \lambda y = 0$ with $\lambda = n(n+1)$. All self-adjointness and orthogonality conclusions are identical.)*

Note that this is a **singular** Sturm–Liouville problem because $p(x) = 1 - x^2 > 0$ on $(-1, 1)$ vanishes at both endpoints $x = \pm 1$. We take the domain of $L$ to consist of functions $y$ that are twice continuously differentiable on $(-1, 1)$ and **regular** at the singular endpoints $x = \pm 1$ (specifically, $y$ and $(1-x^2)y'$ remain bounded with $(1-x^2)y'(x) \to 0$ as $x \to \pm 1$; in particular, all functions in $C^2[-1,1]$, including the polynomial eigenfunctions of the Legendre equation, satisfy this condition, whereas the logarithmically singular second solutions $Q_n(x)$ are excluded).

### 2. Self-adjointness of $L$

Equip the function space on $[-1,1]$ with the Hermitian inner product (corresponding to weight function $w(x) = 1$):
\[
\langle u, v \rangle = \int_{-1}^{1} u^*(x)\,v(x)\,dx.
\]
(For real-valued functions this reduces to $\langle u, v \rangle = \int_{-1}^1 u(x) v(x)\,dx$.)

Let $u$ and $v$ lie in the domain of $L$ (for instance, $u, v \in C^2[-1,1]$, or more generally $u, v$ regular at $x = \pm 1$ so that $(1-x^2)u^* v' \to 0$ and $(1-x^2)(u^*)'v \to 0$ as $x \to \pm 1$). Integrating by parts twice:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle u, Lv \rangle &= -\int_{-1}^{1} u^*(x)\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\left((1 - x^2)v'(x)\right) dx \\
&= -\Big[u^*(x)(1 - x^2)v'(x)\Big]_{-1}^{1} + \int_{-1}^{1} (1 - x^2)(u^*)'(x)\,v'(x)\,dx \\
&= \Big[(1 - x^2)\big((u^*)'(x)\,v(x) - u^*(x)\,v'(x)\big)\Big]_{-1}^{1} - \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{d}{dx}\!\left((1 - x^2)(u^*)'(x)\right) v(x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because $p(x) = 1 - x^2$ is real-valued,
\[
-\frac{d}{dx}\!\left((1 - x^2)(u^*)'(x)\right) = \left(-\frac{d}{dx}\!\left((1 - x^2)u'(x)\right)\right)^* = (Lu)^*(x).
\]
Hence
\[
\langle u, Lv \rangle - \langle Lu, v \rangle = \Big[(1 - x^2)\big((u^*)'(x)\,v(x) - u^*(x)\,v'(x)\big)\Big]_{-1}^{1}.
\]
Since $p(x) = 1 - x^2 = 0$ at $x = \pm 1$ and $u, v$ are regular at $x = \pm 1$ (so $(u^*)'v$ and $u^*v'$ remain bounded, or at least grow more slowly than $(1-x^2)^{-1}$), the boundary term vanishes identically:
\[
\Big[(1 - x^2)\big((u^*)'v - u^* v'\big)\Big]_{-1}^{1} = 0.
\]
*(Notice that regularity at $x = \pm 1$ is essential: if a singular solution such as $v = Q_0(x) = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1+x}{1-x}$ were permitted alongside $u = x$, then $v'(x) = (1-x^2)^{-1}$ and $[(1-x^2)(u'v - uv')]_{-1}^1 = [-x]_{-1}^1 = -2 \neq 0$.)*

Therefore,
\[
\langle u, Lv \rangle = \langle Lu, v \rangle
\]
for all $u, v$ in the domain of $L$, proving that $L$ is **self-adjoint** with respect to the unweighted inner product on $[-1, 1]$.

### 3. Orthogonality relation between the eigenfunctions

Let $y_n(x)$ and $y_m(x)$ be regular eigenfunctions of $L$ on $[-1, 1]$ corresponding to non-negative integers $n$ and $m$:
\[
L y_n = \lambda_n y_n, \qquad L y_m = \lambda_m y_m, \qquad \lambda_n = n(n+1), \quad \lambda_m = m(m+1).
\]
(Note that self-adjointness guarantees all eigenvalues are real: $\lambda_n\langle y_n, y_n\rangle = \langle y_n, Ly_n\rangle = \langle Ly_n, y_n\rangle = \lambda_n^*\langle y_n, y_n\rangle$, and since $y_n \not\equiv 0$ is continuous, $\langle y_n, y_n\rangle > 0 \implies \lambda_n = \lambda_n^*$, which is already evident here since $n(n+1) \in \mathbb{R}$.)

Applying self-adjointness to $u = y_m$ and $v = y_n$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
0 &= \langle y_m, L y_n \rangle - \langle L y_m, y_n \rangle \\
  &= \langle y_m, \lambda_n y_n \rangle - \langle \lambda_m y_m, y_n \rangle \\
  &= (\lambda_n - \lambda_m^*)\,\langle y_m, y_n \rangle \\
  &= \big(n(n+1) - m(m+1)\big) \int_{-1}^{1} y_m^*(x)\,y_n(x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\]
Factoring the difference of eigenvalues gives
\[
\lambda_n - \lambda_m = n(n+1) - m(m+1) = (n - m)(n + m + 1).
\]
Since $n$ and $m$ are non-negative integers, $n + m + 1 \ge 1 > 0$. Consequently, $\lambda_n \neq \lambda_m$ whenever $n \neq m$. Dividing by $\lambda_n - \lambda_m \neq 0$ yields the orthogonality relation:
\[
\int_{-1}^{1} y_m^*(x)\,y_n(x)\,dx = 0 \qquad \text{for } m \neq n
\]
(and for real eigenfunctions $y_n, y_m$, simply $\int_{-1}^{1} y_m(x)\,y_n(x)\,dx = 0$ for $m \neq n$, with weight function $w(x) = 1$).

#### Remark on normalisation for the standard Legendre polynomials $P_n(x)$

Since the question specifies no normalisation for the eigenfunctions, the value of $\int_{-1}^{1} |y_n(x)|^2\,dx$ depends on the chosen scaling of $y_n$. For the standard Legendre polynomials $P_n(x)$ normalised by $P_n(1) = 1$, Rodrigues' formula gives
\[
P_n(x) = \frac{1}{2^n n!}\frac{d^n}{dx^n}\!\left((x^2 - 1)^n\right).
\]
Indeed, setting $f(x) = (x^2-1)^n$ gives $(x^2-1)f' = 2n x f$; differentiating $n+1$ times via Leibniz's rule yields $(x^2-1)f^{(n+2)} + 2x f^{(n+1)} - n(n+1)f^{(n)} = 0$, so $f^{(n)}$ solves Legendre's equation, and writing $(x^2-1)^n = (x-1)^n(x+1)^n$ shows $f^{(n)}(1) = n!\,2^n$, hence $P_n(1) = 1$.

Integrating by parts $n$ times (all boundary terms vanish because $\frac{d^k}{dx^k}(x^2-1)^n$ contains a factor $(x^2-1)^{n-k}$ which vanishes at $x = \pm 1$ for $0 \le k \le n-1$) and using $\frac{d^{2n}}{dx^{2n}}(x^2-1)^n = (2n)!$:
\[
\int_{-1}^{1} P_n(x)^2\,dx = \frac{(-1)^n}{(2^n n!)^2}\int_{-1}^{1} (x^2-1)^n (2n)!\,dx = \frac{(2n)!}{(2^n n!)^2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^2)^n\,dx.
\]
For $I_n = \int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^2)^n\,dx$, integrating by parts once gives
\[
I_n = \Big[x(1-x^2)^n\Big]_{-1}^{1} + 2n\int_{-1}^{1} x^2(1-x^2)^{n-1}\,dx = 2n(I_{n-1} - I_n) \implies I_n = \frac{2n}{2n+1} I_{n-1}.
\]
With $I_0 = 2$, induction gives $I_n = \frac{2^{2n+1}(n!)^2}{(2n+1)!}$, and therefore
\[
\int_{-1}^{1} P_m(x)\,P_n(x)\,dx = \frac{2}{2n+1}\,\delta_{mn}.
\]

## Final Answers

- **Sturm–Liouville eigenvalue form:**
  \[
  Ly = \lambda y, \qquad L y := -\frac{d}{dx}\!\left((1-x^2)\frac{dy}{dx}\right), \qquad \lambda = n(n+1) \quad (n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}),
  \]
  with $p(x) = 1 - x^2$, $q(x) = 0$, and weight $w(x) = 1$ on $[-1, 1]$ (or equivalently $\tilde{L}y = \frac{d}{dx}((1-x^2)y') = -n(n+1)y$).
- **Self-adjointness:** $\langle u, Lv \rangle = \langle Lu, v \rangle$ with respect to $\langle u, v \rangle = \int_{-1}^1 u^*(x)v(x)\,dx$ on functions regular at $x = \pm 1$, because the boundary term $[(1-x^2)((u^*)'v - u^*v')]_{-1}^1$ vanishes identically.
- **Orthogonality relation:**
  \[
  \int_{-1}^{1} y_m^*(x)\,y_n(x)\,dx = 0 \qquad (m \neq n)
  \]
  (for real eigenfunctions, $\int_{-1}^{1} y_m(x) y_n(x)\,dx = 0$ for $m \neq n$). For the standard Legendre polynomials normalised by $P_n(1)=1$, $\int_{-1}^{1} P_m(x) P_n(x)\,dx = \frac{2}{2n+1}\,\delta_{mn}$.
