## Full Solution

We seek the causal Green function $G(t, t')$ for the differential operator $\mathcal{L} = \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}t^2} - k^2$ on $t > 0$ with homogeneous initial conditions $x(0) = 0$, $\dot{x}(0) = 0$.

### Defining problem for $G(t, t')$

Consider the distributional equation (for $t > 0$, $t' > 0$)
$$
\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial t^2}(t, t') - k^2 G(t, t') = \delta(t - t'),
$$
subject to the homogeneous initial conditions
$$
G(0, t') = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(0, t') = 0 \qquad (t' > 0).
$$

1. **Region $0 \le t < t'$ (causality):**
   For $0 < t < t'$, the right-hand side vanishes, so $G(t, t')$ satisfies the homogeneous equation
   $$
   \frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial t^2} - k^2 G = 0
   $$
   with initial data $G(0, t') = 0$ and $\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(0, t') = 0$. By uniqueness of solutions to linear ODE initial value problems,
   $$
   G(t, t') = 0 \qquad \text{for } 0 \le t < t'.
   $$
   In particular, because $G(t, t') = 0$ whenever $t' > t$, the general integral representation $x(t) = \int_0^\infty G(t, t') f(t')\,\mathrm{d}t'$ automatically reduces to the causal integral
   $$
   x(t) = \int_0^t G(t, t') f(t')\,\mathrm{d}t'.
   $$

2. **Matching conditions at $t = t'$:**
   - **Continuity of $G$:** For $\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial t^2}$ to have a $\delta(t - t')$ singularity (and no $\delta'(t - t')$ singularity), $G(t, t')$ must be continuous at $t = t'$. Hence
     $$
     G(t'^+, t') = G(t'^-, t') = 0.
     $$
   - **Jump in $\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}$:** Integrating $\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial t^2} - k^2 G = \delta(t - t')$ over $[t' - \varepsilon, t' + \varepsilon]$ and letting $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ (using continuity of $G$ so that $\int_{t'-\varepsilon}^{t'+\varepsilon} k^2 G\,\mathrm{d}t \to 0$) gives
     $$
     \left[\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(t, t')\right]_{t = t'^-}^{t = t'^+} = 1.
     $$
     Since $\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(t'^-, t') = 0$, this yields
     $$
     \frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(t'^+, t') = 1.
     $$

3. **Region $t > t'$:**
   Here $G(t, t')$ satisfies the homogeneous equation $\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial t^2} - k^2 G = 0$ with initial values at $t = t'^+$ given by $G(t'^+, t') = 0$ and $\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(t'^+, t') = 1$.

   - **Generic case $k \neq 0$:**
     The general solution of $\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial t^2} - k^2 G = 0$ for $t > t'$ can be written in terms of the shifted variable $t - t'$ as
     $$
     G(t, t') = A(t') \cosh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr) + B(t') \sinh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr)
     $$
     (equivalently $C(t') e^{kt} + D(t') e^{-kt}$).
     Applying the matching conditions at $t = t'^+$:
     - $G(t'^+, t') = A(t')\cosh 0 + B(t')\sinh 0 = A(t') = 0$;
     - $\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial t}(t'^+, t') = k B(t')\cosh 0 = k B(t') = 1 \implies B(t') = \dfrac{1}{k}$.

     Hence, for $t > t'$,
     $$
     G(t, t') = \frac{1}{k}\sinh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr) = \frac{e^{k(t - t')} - e^{-k(t - t')}}{2k}.
     $$
     (Notice this expression is even in $k$, so it does not depend on the sign of $k$, and remains valid for complex $k \neq 0$, e.g. $k = i\omega$ gives $\frac{1}{\omega}\sin(\omega(t - t'))$.)

   - **Degenerate case $k = 0$:**
     When $k = 0$, the homogeneous ODE is $\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial t^2} = 0$, whose general solution is $G(t, t') = A(t') + B(t')(t - t')$. Applying $G(t'^+, t') = 0$ and $\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(t'^+, t') = 1$ gives $A(t') = 0$, $B(t') = 1$, so
     $$
     G(t, t') = t - t' \qquad (t > t'),
     $$
     which agrees continuously with $\lim_{k \to 0} \frac{1}{k}\sinh(k(t - t'))$.

Combining both regions using the Heaviside step function $H$, the full causal Green function for $t, t' > 0$ is
$$
G(t, t') = H(t - t')\,\frac{\sinh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr)}{k} = \begin{cases} 0, & 0 \le t < t', \\ \dfrac{1}{k}\sinh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr), & t \ge t', \end{cases}
$$
for $k \neq 0$, and $G(t, t') = (t - t') H(t - t')$ for $k = 0$.

### Direct verification via differentiation under the integral sign

For any continuous forcing function $f(t)$ on $t \ge 0$, define
$$
x(t) = \int_0^t G_+(t, t') f(t')\,\mathrm{d}t', \qquad G_+(t, t') := \frac{1}{k}\sinh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr) \quad (k \neq 0).
$$
Then:
- $x(0) = \int_0^0 G_+(0, t') f(t')\,\mathrm{d}t' = 0$.
- By Leibniz's integral rule, since $G_+(t, t) = 0$,
  $$
  \dot{x}(t) = G_+(t, t) f(t) + \int_0^t \frac{\partial G_+}{\partial t}(t, t') f(t')\,\mathrm{d}t' = \int_0^t \cosh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr) f(t')\,\mathrm{d}t',
  $$
  which gives $\dot{x}(0) = 0$.
- Differentiating once more, since $\left.\frac{\partial G_+}{\partial t}(t, t')\right|_{t'=t} = \cosh 0 = 1$ and $\frac{\partial^2 G_+}{\partial t^2} = k^2 G_+$,
  $$
  \ddot{x}(t) = 1 \cdot f(t) + \int_0^t k^2 G_+(t, t') f(t')\,\mathrm{d}t' = f(t) + k^2 x(t),
  $$
  so $\ddot{x} - k^2 x = f(t)$ for all $t > 0$. (The identical verification holds for $k = 0$ with $G_+(t, t') = t - t'$.)

## Final Answers

- For $k \neq 0$:
  $$
  G(t, t') = \frac{1}{k}\sinh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr) \qquad (0 \le t' \le t),
  $$
  or on the full domain $t, t' > 0$:
  $$
  G(t, t') = H(t - t')\,\frac{\sinh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr)}{k} = \begin{cases} 0, & 0 \le t < t', \\ \dfrac{1}{k}\sinh\bigl(k(t - t')\bigr), & t \ge t'. \end{cases}
  $$
- In the degenerate case $k = 0$: $G(t, t') = (t - t') H(t - t')$ (i.e. $G(t, t') = t - t'$ for $0 \le t' \le t$).
