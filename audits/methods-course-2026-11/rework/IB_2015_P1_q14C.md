## Full Solution

### (i) Sturm–Liouville form and properties of solutions

#### Sturm–Liouville form

Any linear second-order ordinary differential eigenvalue equation for a real-valued function $y(x)$ on an interval $[a, b]$,
\[
\alpha(x)\frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} + \beta(x)\frac{dy}{dx} + \gamma(x) y = \lambda \rho(x) y \qquad (\alpha(x) \neq 0),
\]
can be put into **Sturm–Liouville form** by multiplying through by the integrating factor $I(x) = \frac{1}{\alpha(x)}\exp\!\left(\int \frac{\beta(x)}{\alpha(x)}\,dx\right)$ (so that $p(x) := I(x)\alpha(x)$ satisfies $p'(x) = I(x)\beta(x)$). Writing $q(x) = -I(x)\gamma(x)$ and $w(x) = I(x)\rho(x)$ gives the Sturm–Liouville eigenfunction equation (in the sign convention matching part (iii)):
\[
\mathcal{L} y \equiv \frac{d}{dx}\!\left(p(x)\frac{dy}{dx}\right) - q(x) y = \lambda w(x) y, \qquad \text{equivalently} \quad \tilde{\mathcal{L}} y \equiv \frac{1}{w(x)}\mathcal{L} y = \lambda y, \qquad x \in [a, b],
\]
(equivalently, with $-\lambda$ replacing $\lambda$ in the convention $-(py')' + qy = \mu w y$), where:
- $p, q, w$ are real-valued functions on $[a, b]$ (for a *regular* Sturm–Liouville problem on a finite interval $[a, b]$, $p \in C^1([a,b])$, $q, w \in C([a,b])$, with $p(x) > 0$ and $w(x) > 0$ on $[a, b]$; if $p$ or $w$ vanishes at an endpoint or the interval is infinite, the problem is *singular*);
- $w(x) > 0$ on $(a, b)$ is the **weight function**, defining the weighted inner product
  \[
  \langle u, v \rangle_w = \int_a^b u(x) v(x) w(x)\,dx
  \]
  (or $\int_a^b u(x)^* v(x) w(x)\,dx$ when extended to complex-valued functions);
- $\lambda$ is the **eigenvalue**, and a non-trivial solution $y(x)$ satisfying homogeneous boundary conditions at $x = a, b$ that make the operator self-adjoint (see part (ii)) is an **eigenfunction**.

#### Properties of the solutions (for a regular self-adjoint Sturm–Liouville problem)

1. **Real eigenvalues and real eigenfunctions:** All eigenvalues $\lambda_n$ are real, and the corresponding eigenfunctions $y_n(x)$ can be chosen to be real-valued.
2. **Orthogonality with respect to the weight $w(x)$:** Eigenfunctions $y_n, y_m$ corresponding to distinct eigenvalues $\lambda_n \neq \lambda_m$ are orthogonal with respect to $w(x)$:
   \[
   \langle y_n, y_m \rangle_w = \int_a^b y_n(x) y_m(x) w(x)\,dx = 0 \qquad (\lambda_n \neq \lambda_m).
   \]
   (For separated boundary conditions, every eigenvalue is **simple** / non-degenerate; if degeneracy occurs, e.g. with periodic boundary conditions, Gram–Schmidt produces an orthogonal basis within each eigenspace.)
3. **Orthonormality:** Since $w(x) > 0$, each eigenfunction can be normalised so that $\int_a^b y_n(x) y_m(x) w(x)\,dx = \delta_{nm}$.
4. **Countable discrete spectrum:** There is a countably infinite set of discrete eigenvalues ordered (in the convention $\mathcal{L} y = \lambda w y$ with $p, w > 0$) as $\lambda_1 > \lambda_2 > \lambda_3 > \cdots \to -\infty$ (equivalently bounded below and $\to +\infty$ in the convention $-\mathcal{L} y = \mu w y$). For separated boundary conditions, the $n$-th eigenfunction has $n-1$ simple zeros in $(a, b)$.
5. **Completeness (eigenfunction expansion and Parseval's identity):** The normalised eigenfunctions $\{y_n\}$ form a complete orthonormal basis for $L^2_w([a, b])$. Any square-integrable (e.g. piecewise smooth satisfying the BCs) function $g$ on $[a, b]$ can be expanded as
   \[
   g(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n y_n(x), \qquad c_n = \langle y_n, g \rangle_w = \int_a^b y_n(x) g(x) w(x)\,dx,
   \]
   converging in the weighted mean-square ($L^2_w$) sense, with Parseval's identity $\int_a^b |g(x)|^2 w(x)\,dx = \sum_{n=1}^\infty |c_n|^2$.

### (ii) Self-adjointness condition and boundary conditions

Let $\mathcal{L} y = \frac{d}{dx}\bigl(p(x)y'\bigr) - q(x) y$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{L}} y = \frac{1}{w(x)}\mathcal{L} y$. For real-valued functions $y_1, y_2$, self-adjointness of $\tilde{\mathcal{L}}$ with respect to the weighted inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_w$ requires
\[
\langle y_1, \tilde{\mathcal{L}} y_2 \rangle_w = \langle \tilde{\mathcal{L}} y_1, y_2 \rangle_w \iff \int_a^b y_1(x) (\mathcal{L} y_2)(x)\,dx = \int_a^b (\mathcal{L} y_1)(x)\, y_2(x)\,dx,
\]
i.e. $\int_a^b \bigl(y_1 \mathcal{L} y_2 - y_2 \mathcal{L} y_1\bigr)\,dx = 0$. (For complex-valued functions with $\langle y_1, y_2 \rangle_w = \int_a^b y_1^* y_2 w\,dx$, the same derivation applies with $y_1^*$ in place of $y_1$, since $p, q, w$ are real.)

Integrating by parts twice:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_a^b y_1 \mathcal{L} y_2\,dx &= \int_a^b \left[y_1 \frac{d}{dx}\bigl(p y_2'\bigr) - q y_1 y_2\right] dx \\
&= \Bigl[p(x) y_1(x) y_2'(x)\Bigr]_a^b - \int_a^b \bigl(p y_1' y_2' + q y_1 y_2\bigr)\,dx \\
&= \Bigl[p(x)\bigl(y_1(x) y_2'(x) - y_1'(x) y_2(x)\bigr)\Bigr]_a^b + \int_a^b \left[\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(p y_1'\bigr) - q y_1\right] y_2\,dx \\
&= \Bigl[p(x)\bigl(y_1(x) y_2'(x) - y_1'(x) y_2(x)\bigr)\Bigr]_a^b + \int_a^b (\mathcal{L} y_1)\, y_2\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\]
(Equivalently, this follows by integrating Lagrange's identity $y_1 \mathcal{L} y_2 - y_2 \mathcal{L} y_1 = \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[p(y_1 y_2' - y_1' y_2)\bigr]$ from $a$ to $b$.)

Hence the operator is self-adjoint if and only if for all solutions $y_1, y_2$ satisfying the boundary conditions,
\[
\Bigl[p(x)\bigl(y_1(x) y_2'(x) - y_1'(x) y_2(x)\bigr)\Bigr]_a^b = 0.
\]

#### Types of boundary conditions satisfying this condition

1. **Dirichlet (homogeneous) boundary conditions:** $y(a) = 0$ and $y(b) = 0$.
   Then $y_1(a) = y_2(a) = 0$ and $y_1(b) = y_2(b) = 0$, so $p(y_1 y_2' - y_1' y_2)$ vanishes at both $x = a$ and $x = b$.
2. **Neumann (homogeneous) boundary conditions:** $y'(a) = 0$ and $y'(b) = 0$.
   Then $y_1'(a) = y_2'(a) = 0$ and $y_1'(b) = y_2'(b) = 0$, so $p(y_1 y_2' - y_1' y_2)$ vanishes at both $x = a$ and $x = b$.
3. **Robin / mixed (general separated homogeneous) boundary conditions:**
   $\alpha y'(a) + \beta y(a) = 0$ and $\mu y'(b) + \nu y(b) = 0$, with real constants $(\alpha, \beta) \neq (0, 0)$ and $(\mu, \nu) \neq (0, 0)$.
   At $x = a$, the homogeneous system
   \[
   \begin{pmatrix} y_1'(a) & y_1(a) \\ y_2'(a) & y_2(a) \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \alpha \\ \beta \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   has a non-trivial solution $(\alpha, \beta)^T$, so its determinant vanishes: $y_1'(a) y_2(a) - y_1(a) y_2'(a) = 0$. By the same argument at $x = b$, $y_1'(b) y_2(b) - y_1(b) y_2'(b) = 0$. Thus the boundary term vanishes at each endpoint separately. (This family contains Dirichlet, Neumann, and combinations such as $y(a)=0, y'(b)=0$ as special cases.)
4. **Periodic boundary conditions** (when $p(a) = p(b)$):
   $y(a) = y(b)$ and $y'(a) = y'(b)$.
   Then $p(b)\bigl(y_1(b) y_2'(b) - y_1'(b) y_2(b)\bigr) = p(a)\bigl(y_1(a) y_2'(a) - y_1'(a) y_2(a)\bigr)$, so the endpoint contributions cancel.
5. **Singular / regularity boundary conditions** (when $p(a) = 0$ and/or $p(b) = 0$, e.g. Legendre's equation on $[-1,1]$ with $p(x) = 1 - x^2$):
   Requiring $y$ and $y'$ to remain bounded (regular) at any endpoint where $p$ vanishes (or more generally $p(x)(y_1 y_2' - y_1' y_2) \to 0$ as $x \to a^+, b^-$) ensures the boundary term vanishes.

### (iii) Reduction to a Fredholm integral equation

We are given the inhomogeneous Sturm–Liouville equation on $[a, b]$ (with $w(x) > 0$ so that $\sqrt{w(x)}$ is well-defined and real):
\[
\frac{1}{w(x)}\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(p(x)\frac{dy}{dx}\right) - \frac{q(x)}{w(x)} y - \lambda y = f(x),
\]
and the Green's function $G(x, \xi)$ satisfying
\[
\mathcal{L}_x G(x, \xi) \equiv \frac{d}{dx}\!\left(p(x)\frac{dG}{dx}\right) - q(x) G(x, \xi) = \delta(x - \xi),
\]
where both $y(x)$ and $G(x, \xi)$ (as a function of $x$) satisfy the same separated homogeneous Robin boundary conditions
\[
\alpha u'(a) + \beta u(a) = 0, \qquad \mu u'(b) + \nu u(b) = 0 \qquad \bigl((\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0),\ (\mu,\nu)\neq(0,0)\bigr).
\]

Multiplying the inhomogeneous Sturm–Liouville equation by $w(x)$ rearranges it to
\[
\mathcal{L} y(x) \equiv \frac{d}{dx}\!\left(p(x)\frac{dy}{dx}\right) - q(x) y(x) = w(x)\bigl(f(x) + \lambda y(x)\bigr).
\]

Now apply Green's identity from part (ii) to the functions $y(x)$ and $G(x, \xi)$ (for fixed $\xi \in (a, b)$). To verify the jump contribution rigorously without relying solely on formal distribution rules, note that $G(\cdot, \xi)$ is continuous at $x = \xi$, satisfies $\mathcal{L}_x G(x,\xi) = 0$ for $x \neq \xi$, and integrating $\mathcal{L}_x G = \delta(x-\xi)$ across $(\xi-\epsilon, \xi+\epsilon)$ gives the jump condition
\[
\Bigl[p(x)\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}(x, \xi)\Bigr]_{x=\xi^-}^{x=\xi^+} = 1.
\]
Splitting the integral at $x = \xi$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_a^b G(x,\xi)\mathcal{L} y(x)\,dx &= \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+}\left(\int_a^{\xi-\epsilon} + \int_{\xi+\epsilon}^b\right)\Bigl(G(x,\xi)\mathcal{L} y(x) - y(x)\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_x G(x,\xi)}_{=\,0}\Bigr)\,dx \\
&= \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+}\left(\Bigl[p(x)\bigl(G y' - G_x y\bigr)\Bigr]_a^{\xi-\epsilon} + \Bigl[p(x)\bigl(G y' - G_x y\bigr)\Bigr]_{\xi+\epsilon}^b\right) \\
&= \Bigl[p(x)\bigl(G(x,\xi) y'(x) - G_x(x,\xi) y(x)\bigr)\Bigr]_a^b \\
&\quad + p(\xi)\underbrace{\bigl(G(\xi^-,\xi) - G(\xi^+,\xi)\bigr)}_{=\,0} y'(\xi) + y(\xi)\underbrace{\Bigl[p(\xi)G_x(\xi^+,\xi) - p(\xi)G_x(\xi^-,\xi)\Bigr]}_{=\,1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because $y(x)$ and $G(x, \xi)$ satisfy the same homogeneous Robin boundary conditions at $x = a$ and $x = b$, by part (ii) the boundary term $\bigl[p(G y' - G_x y)\bigr]_a^b$ vanishes identically! (Equivalently, in the distributional sense, $\int_a^b \bigl(G\mathcal{L}y - y\mathcal{L}_x G\bigr)\,dx = \bigl[p(G y' - G_x y)\bigr]_a^b = 0 \implies \int_a^b G(x,\xi)\mathcal{L}y(x)\,dx = \int_a^b y(x)\delta(x-\xi)\,dx = y(\xi)$.)

Hence
\[
y(\xi) = \int_a^b G(x, \xi)\mathcal{L} y(x)\,dx = \int_a^b G(x, \xi)\,w(x)\bigl(f(x) + \lambda y(x)\bigr)\,dx.
\]
Multiplying both sides by $\sqrt{w(\xi)}$ gives
\[
\sqrt{w(\xi)}\,y(\xi) = \int_a^b \sqrt{w(\xi)w(x)}\,G(x, \xi)\,\sqrt{w(x)}\,f(x)\,dx + \lambda \int_a^b \sqrt{w(\xi)w(x)}\,G(x, \xi)\,\sqrt{w(x)}\,y(x)\,dx.
\]
Using the definitions $\psi(x) = \sqrt{w(x)}\,y(x)$ and $K(x, \xi) = \sqrt{w(\xi)w(x)}\,G(x, \xi)$, this takes the desired Fredholm integral equation form
\[
\psi(\xi) = U(\xi) + \lambda \int_a^b K(x, \xi)\psi(x)\,dx,
\]
where
\[
U(\xi) = \int_a^b K(x, \xi)\sqrt{w(x)}\,f(x)\,dx.
\]
(Note also that by self-adjointness of $\mathcal{L}$, $G(x,\xi) = G(\xi,x)$, so the symmetrised kernel $K(x,\xi) = K(\xi,x)$ is symmetric.)

### (iv) Fredholm integral equation for $y'' - \lambda y = 0$ on $[0, 1]$ with $y(0) = y(1) = 0$

Comparing
\[
\frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} - \lambda y = 0, \qquad x \in [0, 1], \qquad y(0) = y(1) = 0,
\]
with the general equation in part (iii), we identify:
\[
[a, b] = [0, 1], \qquad p(x) = 1, \qquad q(x) = 0, \qquad w(x) = 1, \qquad f(x) = 0,
\]
with Dirichlet boundary conditions ($\alpha = 0, \beta = 1$ at $x=0$; $\mu = 0, \nu = 1$ at $x=1$).

Therefore $\psi(x) = y(x)$, $U(\xi) = 0$, and $K(x, \xi) = G(x, \xi)$, where $G(x, \xi)$ satisfies
\[
\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial x^2} = \delta(x - \xi), \qquad 0 < x, \xi < 1, \qquad G(0, \xi) = G(1, \xi) = 0.
\]

Solving for $G(x, \xi)$:
1. For $x \neq \xi$, $\frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial x^2} = 0$, so $G$ is piecewise linear in $x$. Imposing $G(0, \xi) = 0$ and $G(1, \xi) = 0$:
   \[
   G(x, \xi) = \begin{cases} A(\xi)\,x, & 0 \le x < \xi, \\ B(\xi)(1 - x), & \xi < x \le 1. \end{cases}
   \]
2. Continuity at $x = \xi$:
   \[
   A(\xi)\,\xi = B(\xi)(1 - \xi).
   \]
3. Derivative jump condition at $x = \xi$ (with $p(\xi) = +1$):
   \[
   \left.\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}\right|_{x=\xi^+} - \left.\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}\right|_{x=\xi^-} = \frac{1}{p(\xi)} = 1 \implies -B(\xi) - A(\xi) = 1 \implies B(\xi) = -1 - A(\xi).
   \]
4. Solving the two equations:
   \[
   A(\xi)\,\xi = \bigl(-1 - A(\xi)\bigr)(1 - \xi) = -1 + \xi - A(\xi) + A(\xi)\,\xi \implies A(\xi) = -(1 - \xi),
   \]
   and hence $B(\xi) = -1 - \bigl(-(1-\xi)\bigr) = -\xi$.

Thus the Green's function (and kernel) is
\[
K(x, \xi) = G(x, \xi) = \begin{cases} -x(1 - \xi), & 0 \le x \le \xi, \\ -\xi(1 - x), & \xi \le x \le 1. \end{cases}
\]
(Notice $G(x,\xi) = G(\xi,x) \le 0$, consistent with convexity $G'' = +\delta(x-\xi) \ge 0$ and $G(0,\xi) = G(1,\xi) = 0$.)

Substituting into the Fredholm integral equation from part (iii) gives the homogeneous Fredholm integral equation:
\[
y(\xi) = \lambda \int_0^1 K(x, \xi)\,y(x)\,dx, \qquad K(x, \xi) = \begin{cases} -x(1 - \xi), & 0 \le x \le \xi, \\ -\xi(1 - x), & \xi \le x \le 1, \end{cases}
\]
or equivalently, written out explicitly,
\[
y(\xi) = -\lambda\left[(1 - \xi)\int_0^\xi x\,y(x)\,dx + \xi\int_\xi^1 (1 - x)\,y(x)\,dx\right].
\]

**Direct check:** Differentiating twice with respect to $\xi$:
\[
y'(\xi) = -\lambda\left[-\int_0^\xi x\,y(x)\,dx + (1-\xi)\xi y(\xi) + \int_\xi^1 (1-x)\,y(x)\,dx - \xi(1-\xi)y(\xi)\right] = -\lambda\left[-\int_0^\xi x\,y(x)\,dx + \int_\xi^1 (1-x)\,y(x)\,dx\right],
\]
\[
y''(\xi) = -\lambda\bigl[-\xi y(\xi) - (1-\xi)y(\xi)\bigr] = \lambda y(\xi),
\]
and evaluating at $\xi = 0, 1$ immediately yields $y(0) = 0$ and $y(1) = 0$. (For an eigenfunction $y_n(x) = \sin(n\pi x)$, the eigenvalue is $\lambda_n = -n^2\pi^2 < 0$, which combines with the negative kernel $K \le 0$ to reproduce $+y_n(\xi)$.)

## Final Answers

- **(i)** Sturm–Liouville form: $\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(p(x)y'\bigr) - q(x)y = \lambda w(x)y$ on $[a,b]$ with real $p, q, w$ and weight $w > 0$; solutions of a regular self-adjoint problem have real discrete countable eigenvalues ($\lambda_n \to -\infty$ in this sign convention), real eigenfunctions orthogonal with respect to $w(x)$ (normalisable to $\langle y_n, y_m \rangle_w = \delta_{nm}$), and completeness in $L^2_w([a,b])$ (eigenfunction expansion and Parseval's identity).
- **(ii)** Self-adjointness condition: $\Bigl[p(x)\bigl(y_1(x)y_2'(x) - y_1'(x)y_2(x)\bigr)\Bigr]_a^b = 0$. Satisfied by (e.g.) Dirichlet ($y(a)=y(b)=0$), Neumann ($y'(a)=y'(b)=0$), Robin/mixed ($\alpha y'(a)+\beta y(a)=0$, $\mu y'(b)+\nu y(b)=0$), Periodic ($y(a)=y(b), y'(a)=y'(b)$ when $p(a)=p(b)$), and Singular/regularity BCs (when $p$ vanishes at endpoints).
- **(iii)** Fredholm integral equation $\psi(\xi) = U(\xi) + \lambda \int_a^b K(x,\xi)\psi(x)\,dx$ with
  \[
  U(\xi) = \int_a^b K(x, \xi)\sqrt{w(x)}\,f(x)\,dx.
  \]
- **(iv)** Homogeneous Fredholm integral equation:
  \[
  y(\xi) = \lambda \int_0^1 K(x, \xi)\,y(x)\,dx = -\lambda\left[(1 - \xi)\int_0^\xi x\,y(x)\,dx + \xi\int_\xi^1 (1 - x)\,y(x)\,dx\right],
  \]
  with
  \[
  K(x, \xi) = \begin{cases} -x(1 - \xi), & 0 \le x \le \xi, \\ -\xi(1 - x), & \xi \le x \le 1. \end{cases}
  \]
