## Full Solution

### 1. General solution to Laplace's equation in two-dimensional polar coordinates

In plane polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$, Laplace's equation $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$ is
\[
\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial r}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial \theta^2} = 0.
\]
Seeking separated solutions $\phi(r,\theta) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$ yields
\[
\frac{r}{R}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dR}{dr}\right) = -\frac{1}{\Theta}\frac{d^2\Theta}{d\theta^2} = \lambda,
\]
where $\lambda$ is a separation constant. For a single-valued solution on a full angular domain $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$, $\Theta(\theta)$ must be $2\pi$-periodic ($\Theta(\theta+2\pi)=\Theta(\theta)$ and $\Theta'(\theta+2\pi)=\Theta'(\theta)$):
- $\lambda < 0$ gives hyperbolic/exponential functions in $\theta$, which cannot be $2\pi$-periodic unless identically zero.
- $\lambda = 0$ gives $\Theta(\theta) = c_1 + c_2\theta$; $2\pi$-periodicity requires $c_2 = 0$, so $\Theta$ is constant, and $(r R')' = 0$ yields $R(r) = A_0 + B_0 \ln r$. (Note: on a slit domain or sector without $2\pi$-periodicity, multivalued terms $C_0\theta + D_0\theta\ln r$ and non-integer powers $r^{\pm\nu}$ would also be allowed.)
- $\lambda > 0$: setting $\lambda = n^2$, $2\pi$-periodicity of $\cos(n\theta)$ and $\sin(n\theta)$ restricts $n$ to the positive integers $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$. The radial equation $r^2 R'' + r R' - n^2 R = 0$ is of Euler–Cauchy type with general solution $R(r) = c_n r^n + d_n r^{-n}$.

By linearity and superposition, the general single-valued ($2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$) solution to Laplace's equation in two-dimensional polar coordinates is
\[
\phi(r,\theta) = A_0 + B_0 \ln r + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left[\left(A_n r^n + B_n r^{-n}\right)\cos n\theta + \left(C_n r^n + D_n r^{-n}\right)\sin n\theta\right],
\]
where $A_0, B_0, A_n, B_n, C_n, D_n$ are arbitrary constants.

### 2. Solution in $0 < r < 1$ and $1 < r < \infty$

Let $\phi_-(r,\theta)$ denote the solution for $0 < r < 1$ and $\phi_+(r,\theta)$ denote the solution for $1 < r < \infty$. Since both regions contain all angles $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$, both solutions are single-valued ($2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$) and admit the expansions
\[
\phi_-(r,\theta) = A_0^- + B_0^- \ln r + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\left(A_n^- r^n + B_n^- r^{-n}\right)\cos n\theta + \left(C_n^- r^n + D_n^- r^{-n}\right)\sin n\theta\right], \qquad 0 < r < 1,
\]
\[
\phi_+(r,\theta) = A_0^+ + B_0^+ \ln r + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\left(A_n^+ r^n + B_n^+ r^{-n}\right)\cos n\theta + \left(C_n^+ r^n + D_n^+ r^{-n}\right)\sin n\theta\right], \qquad 1 < r < \infty.
\]

**Step 1: Asymptotic condition as $r \to 0$.**
We require $\phi_-(r,\theta) \to 0$ as $r \to 0^+$. Projecting onto the orthogonal Fourier basis $\{1, \cos n\theta, \sin n\theta\}$ on $[0, 2\pi]$:
- $A_0^- + B_0^- \ln r = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \phi_-(r,\theta)\,d\theta \to 0$ as $r \to 0^+$. Because $\ln r \to -\infty$, this forces $B_0^- = 0$ and then $A_0^- = 0$.
- For each $n \ge 1$, $A_n^- r^n + B_n^- r^{-n} \to 0$ and $C_n^- r^n + D_n^- r^{-n} \to 0$ as $r \to 0^+$. Since $r^n \to 0$ while $r^{-n} \to +\infty$, this forces $B_n^- = 0$ and $D_n^- = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$.

Hence, for $0 < r < 1$,
\[
\phi_-(r,\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} r^n \left(A_n^- \cos n\theta + C_n^- \sin n\theta\right).
\]

**Step 2: Asymptotic condition as $r \to \infty$.**
We require $\phi_+(r,\theta) \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$. Again projecting onto each Fourier mode:
- $A_0^+ + B_0^+ \ln r \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$ forces $B_0^+ = 0$ and $A_0^+ = 0$.
- For each $n \ge 1$, $A_n^+ r^n + B_n^+ r^{-n} \to 0$ and $C_n^+ r^n + D_n^+ r^{-n} \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$. Since $r^n \to +\infty$ while $r^{-n} \to 0$, this forces $A_n^+ = 0$ and $C_n^+ = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$.

Hence, for $1 < r < \infty$,
\[
\phi_+(r,\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} r^{-n} \left(B_n^+ \cos n\theta + D_n^+ \sin n\theta\right).
\]

**Step 3: Continuity at $r = 1$.**
The condition $\left.\phi\right|_{r=1+} = \left.\phi\right|_{r=1-}$ requires
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(B_n^+ \cos n\theta + D_n^+ \sin n\theta\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(A_n^- \cos n\theta + C_n^- \sin n\theta\right)
\]
for all $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$. By orthogonality (uniqueness of Fourier coefficients), for all $n \ge 1$,
\[
B_n^+ = A_n^- =: a_n, \qquad D_n^+ = C_n^- =: b_n.
\]

**Step 4: Radial derivative jump condition at $r = 1$.**
Differentiating with respect to $r$:
\[
\frac{\partial \phi_-}{\partial r} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n r^{n-1} \left(a_n \cos n\theta + b_n \sin n\theta\right), \qquad 0 < r < 1,
\]
\[
\frac{\partial \phi_+}{\partial r} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-n) r^{-n-1} \left(a_n \cos n\theta + b_n \sin n\theta\right), \qquad 1 < r < \infty.
\]
Evaluating the one-sided limits at $r = 1$ and taking their difference (`1+` minus `1-`):
\[
\left.\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial r}\right|_{r=1+} - \left.\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial r}\right|_{r=1-}
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-n - n)\left(a_n \cos n\theta + b_n \sin n\theta\right)
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-2n)\left(a_n \cos n\theta + b_n \sin n\theta\right).
\]
Equating this to $\cos 2\theta + \cos 4\theta$ and matching Fourier coefficients of the linearly independent orthogonal functions $\cos n\theta$ and $\sin n\theta$:
- $b_n = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$;
- $a_n = 0$ for all $n \notin \{2, 4\}$;
- for $n = 2$: $-2(2) a_2 = 1 \implies -4 a_2 = 1 \implies a_2 = -\dfrac{1}{4}$;
- for $n = 4$: $-2(4) a_4 = 1 \implies -8 a_4 = 1 \implies a_4 = -\dfrac{1}{8}$.

Therefore the unique solution is
\[
\phi(r,\theta) = \begin{cases}
-\dfrac{1}{4} r^2 \cos 2\theta - \dfrac{1}{8} r^4 \cos 4\theta, & 0 < r < 1, \\[10pt]
-\dfrac{1}{4} r^{-2} \cos 2\theta - \dfrac{1}{8} r^{-4} \cos 4\theta, & 1 < r < \infty.
\end{cases}
\]

**Direct verification:** Each term $r^{\pm n}\cos n\theta$ satisfies $\nabla^2(r^{\pm n}\cos n\theta) = 0$; $\phi \to 0$ uniformly as $r\to 0$ and $r\to\infty$; $\phi|_{r=1+} = \phi|_{r=1-} = -\frac{1}{4}\cos 2\theta - \frac{1}{8}\cos 4\theta$; and
\[
\left.\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial r}\right|_{r=1+} - \left.\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial r}\right|_{r=1-}
= \left(\frac{1}{2}\cos 2\theta + \frac{1}{2}\cos 4\theta\right) - \left(-\frac{1}{2}\cos 2\theta - \frac{1}{2}\cos 4\theta\right)
= \cos 2\theta + \cos 4\theta.
\]
