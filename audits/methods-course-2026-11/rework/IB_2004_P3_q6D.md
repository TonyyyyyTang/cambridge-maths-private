## Full Solution

Implicit in the formula for $x_c(t)$ is the non-resonance assumption $\sin\omega T \neq 0$ (so $\omega T \notin \pi\mathbb{Z}$, and in particular $\omega \neq 0$ and $T \neq 0$).

### Part 1: First variation $\delta S = 0$ at $x = x_c$

Let $x \in C^2([0, T])$ satisfy the boundary conditions $x(0) = a$ and $x(T) = b$, and let $\delta x \in C^1([0, T])$ be an arbitrary variation vanishing at the endpoints, $\delta x(0) = \delta x(T) = 0$. Writing $\delta x(t) = \varepsilon\eta(t)$ with $\eta \in C^1([0, T])$ and $\eta(0) = \eta(T) = 0$, we expand $S[x + \delta x]$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S[x + \delta x] &= \int_0^T \frac{1}{2}\Bigl((\dot{x} + \delta\dot{x})^2 - \omega^2 (x + \delta x)^2\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t \\
&= S[x] + \int_0^T \bigl(\dot{x}\,\delta\dot{x} - \omega^2 x\,\delta x\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t + \int_0^T \frac{1}{2}\bigl((\delta\dot{x})^2 - \omega^2(\delta x)^2\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first variation $\delta S$ (the part linear in $\delta x$, equivalently $\varepsilon \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}S[x + \varepsilon\eta]\right|_{\varepsilon=0}$) is therefore
$$
\delta S = \int_0^T \bigl(\dot{x}\,\delta\dot{x} - \omega^2 x\,\delta x\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t.
$$
Since $\delta\dot{x} = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\delta x)$, integrating the first term by parts yields
$$
\int_0^T \dot{x}\,\delta\dot{x}\,\mathrm{d}t = \bigl[\dot{x}\,\delta x\bigr]_0^T - \int_0^T \ddot{x}\,\delta x\,\mathrm{d}t.
$$
Because $\delta x(0) = \delta x(T) = 0$, the boundary term vanishes:
$$
\bigl[\dot{x}\,\delta x\bigr]_0^T = \dot{x}(T)\delta x(T) - \dot{x}(0)\delta x(0) = 0.
$$
Hence
$$
\delta S = -\int_0^T \bigl(\ddot{x} + \omega^2 x\bigr)\,\delta x\,\mathrm{d}t.
$$
By the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations, $\delta S = 0$ for all smooth variations $\delta x$ with $\delta x(0) = \delta x(T) = 0$ if and only if $x$ satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation
$$
\ddot{x} + \omega^2 x = 0 \qquad \text{for } t \in [0, T].
$$

We solve this boundary value problem subject to $x(0) = a$, $x(T) = b$ (assuming $\sin\omega T \neq 0$). The general solution of $\ddot{x} + \omega^2 x = 0$ is
$$
x(t) = A\cos\omega t + B\sin\omega t.
$$
Applying the boundary conditions:
- $x(0) = a \implies A = a$;
- $x(T) = b \implies a\cos\omega T + B\sin\omega T = b \implies B = \dfrac{b - a\cos\omega T}{\sin\omega T}$ (valid since $\sin\omega T \neq 0$).

Therefore, the unique stationary path $x = x_c$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_c(t) &= a\cos\omega t + \frac{b - a\cos\omega T}{\sin\omega T}\sin\omega t \\
&= \frac{1}{\sin\omega T}\Bigl[a\bigl(\sin\omega T\cos\omega t - \cos\omega T\sin\omega t\bigr) + b\sin\omega t\Bigr] \\
&= \frac{1}{\sin\omega T}\Bigl[a\sin\omega(T - t) + b\sin\omega t\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
Directly checking $x_c(t)$:
- $x_c(0) = \frac{1}{\sin\omega T}[a\sin\omega T + 0] = a$,
- $x_c(T) = \frac{1}{\sin\omega T}[0 + b\sin\omega T] = b$,
- $\dot{x}_c(t) = \frac{\omega}{\sin\omega T}[-a\cos\omega(T - t) + b\cos\omega t]$,
- $\ddot{x}_c(t) = \frac{\omega^2}{\sin\omega T}[-a\sin\omega(T - t) - b\sin\omega t] = -\omega^2 x_c(t)$.

Thus $\ddot{x}_c + \omega^2 x_c = 0$ identically on $[0, T]$, and substituting $x = x_c$ into $\delta S = -\int_0^T (\ddot{x} + \omega^2 x)\,\delta x\,\mathrm{d}t$ gives $\delta S = 0$ for every variation $\delta x$ with $\delta x(0) = \delta x(T) = 0$.

### Part 2: Evaluating $S[x_c]$ via integration by parts

For $x = x_c$, write
$$
S[x_c] = \int_0^T \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\dot{x}_c^2 - \omega^2 x_c^2\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t.
$$
Integrating the $\dot{x}_c^2$ term by parts gives
$$
\int_0^T \frac{1}{2}\dot{x}_c^2\,\mathrm{d}t = \Bigl[\tfrac{1}{2}x_c\dot{x}_c\Bigr]_0^T - \int_0^T \frac{1}{2}x_c\ddot{x}_c\,\mathrm{d}t.
$$
Combining with the potential term:
$$
S[x_c] = \Bigl[\tfrac{1}{2}x_c\dot{x}_c\Bigr]_0^T - \frac{1}{2}\int_0^T x_c\bigl(\ddot{x}_c + \omega^2 x_c\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t.
$$
Since $\ddot{x}_c + \omega^2 x_c = 0$ on $[0, T]$, the remaining integral vanishes identically, leaving
$$
S[x_c] = \Bigl[\tfrac{1}{2}x_c\dot{x}_c\Bigr]_0^T = \frac{1}{2}\Bigl(x_c(T)\dot{x}_c(T) - x_c(0)\dot{x}_c(0)\Bigr).
$$
From $\dot{x}_c(t) = \dfrac{\omega}{\sin\omega T}\bigl[-a\cos\omega(T - t) + b\cos\omega t\bigr]$, we evaluate at the endpoints:
- At $t = T$: $x_c(T) = b$ and $\dot{x}_c(T) = \dfrac{\omega}{\sin\omega T}\bigl(-a + b\cos\omega T\bigr)$.
- At $t = 0$: $x_c(0) = a$ and $\dot{x}_c(0) = \dfrac{\omega}{\sin\omega T}\bigl(-a\cos\omega T + b\bigr)$.

Substituting these values gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
S[x_c] &= \frac{\omega}{2\sin\omega T}\Bigl[b\bigl(b\cos\omega T - a\bigr) - a\bigl(b - a\cos\omega T\bigr)\Bigr] \\
&= \frac{\omega}{2\sin\omega T}\Bigl[b^2\cos\omega T - ab - ab + a^2\cos\omega T\Bigr] \\
&= \frac{\omega}{2\sin\omega T}\Bigl[(a^2 + b^2)\cos\omega T - 2ab\Bigr],
\end{aligned}
$$
as required.

*(Remark on limiting/degenerate cases: as $\omega \to 0$, $x_c(t) \to a + (b-a)t/T$ and $S[x_c] \to \frac{(b-a)^2}{2T}$, the free-particle action. If $\omega T = n\pi$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$, the homogeneous BVP has nontrivial solutions $C\sin\omega t$, so a stationary path exists only if $b = (-1)^n a$ and is then non-unique.)*
