### Part (i): Lommel integral, orthogonality, and normalization of $J_0(\gamma_k x)$

Let $\alpha, \beta > 0$ with $\alpha \neq \beta$, and define $u(x) = J_0(\alpha x)$ and $v(x) = J_0(\beta x)$. By the given differential equation, $u$ and $v$ satisfy the Sturm–Liouville equations
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(x\frac{du}{dx}\right) + \alpha^2 x u = 0,
\qquad
\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(x\frac{dv}{dx}\right) + \beta^2 x v = 0.
\]
Multiply the first equation by $v(x)$, the second by $u(x)$, and subtract:
\[
v\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(x\frac{du}{dx}\right) - u\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(x\frac{dv}{dx}\right) + (\alpha^2 - \beta^2) x u v = 0.
\]
Using Lagrange's identity
\[
v\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(x\frac{du}{dx}\right) - u\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(x\frac{dv}{dx}\right)
= \frac{d}{dx}\!\left[x\left(v\frac{du}{dx} - u\frac{dv}{dx}\right)\right],
\]
we rewrite this as
\[
(\alpha^2 - \beta^2) x u(x) v(x) = \frac{d}{dx}\!\left[x\left(u\frac{dv}{dx} - v\frac{du}{dx}\right)\right].
\]
Integrating from $x = 0$ to $x = 1$:
\[
(\alpha^2 - \beta^2)\int_0^1 J_0(\alpha x)J_0(\beta x)\,x\,dx
= \left[x\Big(\beta J_0(\alpha x)J_0'(\beta x) - \alpha J_0(\beta x)J_0'(\alpha x)\Big)\right]_0^1,
\]
where we used $\frac{d}{dx}J_0(\alpha x) = \alpha J_0'(\alpha x)$ and $\frac{d}{dx}J_0(\beta x) = \beta J_0'(\beta x)$. Because $J_0$ is regular (smooth) at the origin, $J_0$ and $J_0'$ are finite at $x = 0$, so the boundary term vanishes at the lower limit $x = 0$. Evaluating at $x = 1$ gives
\[
(\alpha^2 - \beta^2)\int_0^1 J_0(\alpha x)J_0(\beta x)\,x\,dx = \beta J_0(\alpha)J_0'(\beta) - \alpha J_0(\beta)J_0'(\alpha).
\]
Since $\alpha, \beta > 0$ and $\alpha \neq \beta$, we have $\alpha^2 - \beta^2 = (\alpha-\beta)(\alpha+\beta) \neq 0$, and dividing yields
\[
\int_0^1 J_0(\alpha x)J_0(\beta x)\,x\,dx = \frac{\beta J_0(\alpha)J_0'(\beta) - \alpha J_0(\beta)J_0'(\alpha)}{\alpha^2 - \beta^2}, \qquad \alpha \neq \beta.
\]

**Deduction of orthogonality ($k \neq \ell$):**
Let $\gamma_1 < \gamma_2 < \cdots$ be the distinct positive zeros of $J_0$, so $J_0(\gamma_k) = 0$ for all $k \ge 1$. For $k \neq \ell$, we have $\gamma_k \neq \gamma_\ell$ and $J_0(\gamma_k) = J_0(\gamma_\ell) = 0$. Setting $\alpha = \gamma_k$ and $\beta = \gamma_\ell$ in the identity above makes the numerator vanish while the denominator $\gamma_k^2 - \gamma_\ell^2 \neq 0$, giving
\[
\int_0^1 J_0(\gamma_k x)J_0(\gamma_\ell x)\,x\,dx = 0, \qquad k \neq \ell.
\]

**Deduction of the normalization integral ($k = \ell$):**
Following the hint, set $\alpha = \gamma_k$ and $\beta = \gamma_k + \epsilon$ with $\epsilon \neq 0$ small ($|\epsilon| < \gamma_k$ so $\beta > 0$). Since $J_0(\gamma_k) = 0$, the first term in the numerator vanishes identically:
\[
\int_0^1 J_0(\gamma_k x)J_0((\gamma_k + \epsilon)x)\,x\,dx
= \frac{-\gamma_k J_0(\gamma_k + \epsilon)J_0'(\gamma_k)}{\gamma_k^2 - (\gamma_k + \epsilon)^2}.
\]
Expanding numerator and denominator for small $\epsilon$, using Taylor's theorem $J_0(\gamma_k + \epsilon) = J_0(\gamma_k) + \epsilon J_0'(\gamma_k) + O(\epsilon^2) = \epsilon J_0'(\gamma_k) + O(\epsilon^2)$:
\[
\int_0^1 J_0(\gamma_k x)J_0((\gamma_k + \epsilon)x)\,x\,dx
= \frac{-\gamma_k\big(\epsilon J_0'(\gamma_k) + O(\epsilon^2)\big)J_0'(\gamma_k)}{-2\gamma_k\epsilon - \epsilon^2}
= \frac{\gamma_k (J_0'(\gamma_k))^2 + O(\epsilon)}{2\gamma_k + \epsilon}.
\]
Taking the limit $\epsilon \to 0$ (passing the limit inside the integral on the left-hand side is justified since $J_0((\gamma_k+\epsilon)x) \to J_0(\gamma_k x)$ uniformly on $[0,1]$ by continuity of $J_0$ on compact sets) yields
\[
\int_0^1 \big(J_0(\gamma_k x)\big)^2\,x\,dx = \frac{\gamma_k (J_0'(\gamma_k))^2}{2\gamma_k} = \frac{1}{2}\big(J_0'(\gamma_k)\big)^2.
\]
*(Remark: since $J_0(\gamma_k x)$ is not identically zero on $[0,1]$, the left-hand side is strictly positive, which automatically shows $J_0'(\gamma_k) \neq 0$ for every $k \ge 1$ — i.e., all positive zeros of $J_0$ are simple.)*

### Part (ii): Motion of the circular drum membrane

The radial displacement $z(r, t)$ on $0 \le r \le 1$, $t \ge 0$ satisfies
\[
\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\frac{\partial z}{\partial r}\right) = \frac{\partial^2 z}{\partial t^2}, \qquad z(1, t) = 0,
\]
with $z(r, t)$ regular (finite) at the centre $r = 0$, and initial conditions
\[
z(r, 0) = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial z}{\partial t}(r, 0) = g(r) = \begin{cases} U, & 0 \le r < b, \\ 0, & b < r \le 1, \end{cases}
\]
where $U$ and $0 < b < 1$ are constants.

#### Step 1: Separation of variables and eigenvalue sign check

Seek non-trivial separated solutions $z(r, t) = R(r)T(t)$. Substituting into the PDE and dividing by $R(r)T(t)$ gives
\[
\frac{1}{r R(r)}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r\frac{dR}{dr}\right) = \frac{T''(t)}{T(t)} = -\lambda,
\]
for a separation constant $\lambda$, leading to
\[
\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r\frac{dR}{dr}\right) + \lambda r R = 0, \qquad T''(t) + \lambda T(t) = 0,
\]
with $R(r)$ regular at $r = 0$ and $R(1) = 0$.

Multiplying the radial equation by $\overline{R}(r)$ and integrating over $[0,1]$ gives, after integration by parts,
\[
\lambda \int_0^1 r |R(r)|^2\,dr = -\int_0^1 \overline{R}(r R')'\,dr = \Big[-r \overline{R} R'\Big]_0^1 + \int_0^1 r |R'(r)|^2\,dr = \int_0^1 r |R'(r)|^2\,dr \ge 0,
\]
since $r\overline{R}R' \to 0$ as $r \to 0^+$ (by regularity at the origin) and $R(1) = 0$. As $R \not\equiv 0$, $\int_0^1 r |R|^2\,dr > 0$, so $\lambda$ must be real and $\lambda \ge 0$. Moreover, if $\lambda = 0$, then $\int_0^1 r |R'|^2\,dr = 0 \implies R'(r) \equiv 0 \implies R(r) = \text{const}$, and $R(1) = 0$ forces $R \equiv 0$ (equivalently, $(rR')' = 0 \implies R(r) = A\ln r + B$; regularity at $r=0$ sets $A = 0$ and $R(1)=0$ sets $B = 0$).

Hence we must have $\lambda > 0$. Writing $\lambda = \alpha^2$ with $\alpha > 0$, the radial ODE is
\[
\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r\frac{dR}{dr}\right) + \alpha^2 r R = 0.
\]
By part (i), the solution regular at $r = 0$ is $R(r) = C J_0(\alpha r)$. The boundary condition $R(1) = 0$ (with $C \neq 0$) requires $J_0(\alpha) = 0$ with $\alpha > 0$, so
\[
\alpha = \gamma_k, \qquad R_k(r) = J_0(\gamma_k r), \qquad k = 1, 2, 3, \ldots.
\]

#### Step 2: Time dependence and superposition

For each $k \ge 1$, $T_k'' + \gamma_k^2 T_k = 0$ has general solution
\[
T_k(t) = A_k \cos(\gamma_k t) + C_k \sin(\gamma_k t).
\]
By linearity and superposition, the general solution regular at $r = 0$ and satisfying $z(1, t) = 0$ is
\[
z(r, t) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} J_0(\gamma_k r)\big[A_k \cos(\gamma_k t) + C_k \sin(\gamma_k t)\big].
\]

#### Step 3: Imposing the initial conditions

1. **Initial displacement $z(r, 0) = 0$:**
   \[
   z(r, 0) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k J_0(\gamma_k r) = 0, \qquad 0 \le r \le 1.
   \]
   Multiplying by $r J_0(\gamma_\ell r)$ and integrating over $[0,1]$, the orthogonality and normalization relations from part (i) yield
   \[
   \frac{1}{2} A_\ell \big(J_0'(\gamma_\ell)\big)^2 = 0 \implies A_\ell = 0 \qquad (\ell = 1, 2, \ldots),
   \]
   since $J_0'(\gamma_\ell) \neq 0$. Thus
   \[
   z(r, t) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} C_k J_0(\gamma_k r)\sin(\gamma_k t).
   \]

2. **Initial velocity $\frac{\partial z}{\partial t}(r, 0) = g(r)$:**
   Differentiating term-by-term with respect to $t$ and setting $t = 0$:
   \[
   \frac{\partial z}{\partial t}(r, 0) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} C_k \gamma_k J_0(\gamma_k r) = g(r) = \begin{cases} U, & r < b, \\ 0, & r > b. \end{cases}
   \]
   Multiplying both sides by $r J_0(\gamma_\ell r)$ and integrating over $[0,1]$, using the orthogonality and normalization identities from part (i) (and relabelling $\ell \to k$):
   \[
   C_k \gamma_k \cdot \frac{1}{2}\big(J_0'(\gamma_k)\big)^2
   = \int_0^1 g(r)\,J_0(\gamma_k r)\,r\,dr
   = U \int_0^b r J_0(\gamma_k r)\,dr.
   \]

#### Step 4: Evaluating $\int_0^b r J_0(\gamma_k r)\,dr$ directly from the ODE

From the Bessel ODE in part (i) with $F(r) = J_0(\gamma_k r)$ and $\alpha = \gamma_k$:
\[
\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r\frac{d}{dr}J_0(\gamma_k r)\right) + \gamma_k^2 r J_0(\gamma_k r) = 0
\implies r J_0(\gamma_k r) = -\frac{1}{\gamma_k^2}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r \gamma_k J_0'(\gamma_k r)\right)
= -\frac{1}{\gamma_k}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r J_0'(\gamma_k r)\right).
\]
Integrating from $r = 0$ to $r = b$ (and using regularity at $r = 0$ so $\left.r J_0'(\gamma_k r)\right|_{r=0} = 0$):
\[
\int_0^b r J_0(\gamma_k r)\,dr = -\frac{1}{\gamma_k}\Big[r J_0'(\gamma_k r)\Big]_0^b = -\frac{b}{\gamma_k} J_0'(\gamma_k b).
\]
Substituting this into the equation for $C_k$:
\[
\frac{1}{2} C_k \gamma_k \big(J_0'(\gamma_k)\big)^2 = -\frac{b U}{\gamma_k} J_0'(\gamma_k b).
\]
Solving for $C_k$ (valid since $\gamma_k > 0$ and $J_0'(\gamma_k) \neq 0$) gives
\[
C_k = -2bU\frac{J_0'(\gamma_k b)}{\gamma_k^2 \big(J_0'(\gamma_k)\big)^2}.
\]
Hence the subsequent motion of the drum membrane is
\[
z(r, t) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} C_k J_0(\gamma_k r)\sin(\gamma_k t), \qquad C_k = -2bU\frac{J_0'(\gamma_k b)}{\gamma_k^2 \big(J_0'(\gamma_k)\big)^2},
\]
as required.
