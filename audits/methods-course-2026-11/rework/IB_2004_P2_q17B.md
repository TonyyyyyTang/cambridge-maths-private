## Full Solution

### 1. Parallel-axis theorem for the moment-of-inertia tensor

Let the rigid body occupy a region $V$ with mass density $\rho(\mathbf{x})$ and total mass $M = \int_V \rho\,dV$. Choose Cartesian coordinates $x_i$ ($i=1,2,3$) with origin at the centre of mass $G$, so that the centre-of-mass condition reads
\[
\int_V \rho x_i \, dV = 0 \qquad (i = 1, 2, 3).
\]
(For a discrete system of point masses $m_\alpha$, every integral $\int_V \rho(\cdot)\,dV$ is replaced identically by $\sum_\alpha m_\alpha (\cdot)$.)

Let $\mathbf{X} = \overrightarrow{GP}$ have components $X_i$. Then a mass element at position $\mathbf{r}$ relative to $G$ (components $x_i$) has position vector relative to $P$ given by
\[
\mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{X}, \qquad r'_i = x_i - X_i.
\]
By definition, the moment-of-inertia tensor relative to $P$ is
\[
I_{ij}(P) = \int_V \rho \left(r'_k r'_k \delta_{ij} - r'_i r'_j\right) dV,
\]
and similarly relative to $G$,
\[
I_{ij}(G) = \int_V \rho \left(x_k x_k \delta_{ij} - x_i x_j\right) dV.
\]
Expanding $r'_k r'_k$ and $r'_i r'_j$ using $r'_i = x_i - X_i$:
\[
r'_k r'_k = (x_k - X_k)(x_k - X_k) = x_k x_k - 2 x_k X_k + X_k X_k,
\]
\[
r'_i r'_j = (x_i - X_i)(x_j - X_j) = x_i x_j - x_i X_j - X_i x_j + X_i X_j.
\]
Substituting into the integral for $I_{ij}(P)$ and pulling the constants $X_i$ outside the integrals:
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{ij}(P)
&= \int_V \rho \left(x_k x_k \delta_{ij} - x_i x_j\right) dV \\
&\quad - 2 X_k \delta_{ij} \int_V \rho x_k \, dV + X_j \int_V \rho x_i \, dV + X_i \int_V \rho x_j \, dV \\
&\quad + \left(X_k X_k \delta_{ij} - X_i X_j\right) \int_V \rho \, dV.
\end{aligned}
\]
By the centre-of-mass condition $\int_V \rho x_k \, dV = 0$, all three cross-term integrals vanish identically. Using $\int_V \rho\,dV = M$ and the definition of $I_{ij}(G)$, we obtain
\[
I_{ij}(P) = I_{ij}(G) + M \left(X_k X_k \delta_{ij} - X_i X_j\right).
\]

### 2. Inertia tensor of the uniform cube about $G$ and about $P = (a, a, a)$

Take the uniform cube of side $2a$ and constant density $\rho$ centred at the origin $G = (0,0,0)$ to occupy $V = [-a, a]^3$ (aligned with the coordinate axes so that $P = (a,a,a)$ is a corner; as shown below, $I_{ij}(G)$ is isotropic so $I_{ij}(G)$ and $I_{ij}(P)$ are in fact independent of any rotation of the cube about $G$ that preserves $P$ as a corner).

The volume of the cube is $(2a)^3 = 8a^3$, so its mass is
\[
M = 8 \rho a^3 \iff \rho = \frac{M}{8a^3}.
\]
We evaluate the components of $I_{ij}(G) = \rho \int_{-a}^a \int_{-a}^a \int_{-a}^a (x_k x_k \delta_{ij} - x_i x_j)\,dx_1\,dx_2\,dx_3$:

- **Off-diagonal components ($i \neq j$):** The integrand $-x_i x_j$ is odd in $x_i$ (and in $x_j$) over the symmetric interval $[-a, a]$, so
  \[
  I_{ij}(G) = -\rho \int_{-a}^a \int_{-a}^a \int_{-a}^a x_i x_j \, dx_1\,dx_2\,dx_3 = 0 \qquad (i \neq j).
  \]
- **Diagonal components ($i = j$):** By symmetry among $x_1, x_2, x_3$,
  \[
  \int_{-a}^a \int_{-a}^a \int_{-a}^a x_1^2 \, dx_1\,dx_2\,dx_3
  = \left[\frac{x_1^3}{3}\right]_{-a}^a (2a)(2a)
  = \frac{2a^3}{3}\cdot 4a^2
  = \frac{8}{3} a^5,
  \]
  and likewise for $x_2^2$ and $x_3^2$. Therefore, for each $i \in \{1,2,3\}$ (e.g. $I_{11}(G) = \rho\int_V (x_2^2 + x_3^2)\,dV$),
  \[
  I_{11}(G) = I_{22}(G) = I_{33}(G) = \rho\left(\frac{8}{3}a^5 + \frac{8}{3}a^5\right) = \frac{16}{3}\rho a^5 = \frac{16}{3}\cdot\frac{M}{8a^3}\cdot a^5 = \frac{2}{3} M a^2.
  \]

Thus the inertia tensor about the centre of mass $G$ is
\[
I_{ij}(G) = \frac{2}{3} M a^2 \delta_{ij} = \frac{2}{3} M a^2 \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\qquad \left(= \frac{16}{3}\rho a^5 \delta_{ij}\right).
\]

Now apply the parallel-axis theorem to find $I_{ij}(P)$ at the corner $P = (a, a, a)$. Here $\mathbf{X} = \overrightarrow{GP} = (a, a, a)$, so $X_1 = X_2 = X_3 = a$, giving
\[
X_k X_k = a^2 + a^2 + a^2 = 3a^2, \qquad X_i X_j = a^2 \quad \text{for all } i, j \in \{1, 2, 3\}.
\]
Substituting into $I_{ij}(P) = I_{ij}(G) + M(X_k X_k \delta_{ij} - X_i X_j)$:
\[
I_{ij}(P) = \frac{2}{3} M a^2 \delta_{ij} + M(3a^2 \delta_{ij} - X_i X_j) = \frac{11}{3} M a^2 \delta_{ij} - M X_i X_j.
\]
Specifically:
- For $i = j$: $I_{ii}(P) = \left(\frac{11}{3} - 1\right) M a^2 = \frac{8}{3} M a^2$.
- For $i \neq j$: $I_{ij}(P) = -M a^2$.

In matrix form:
\[
I(P) = M a^2 \begin{pmatrix} \frac{8}{3} & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & \frac{8}{3} & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & \frac{8}{3} \end{pmatrix}
     = \frac{M a^2}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 8 & -3 & -3 \\ -3 & 8 & -3 \\ -3 & -3 & 8 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

### 3. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $I_{ij}(P)$

Using the rank-one-perturbation form $I_{ij}(P) = \frac{11}{3} M a^2 \delta_{ij} - M X_i X_j$ with $\mathbf{X} = (a,a,a)^T$ and $|\mathbf{X}|^2 = 3a^2$, the action on any vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is
\[
I(P)\mathbf{v} = \frac{11}{3} M a^2 \mathbf{v} - M (\mathbf{X}\cdot\mathbf{v})\mathbf{X}.
\]

1. **Along the body diagonal $\mathbf{X} \parallel (1,1,1)^T$:**
   Taking $\mathbf{v}_1 = (1, 1, 1)^T$ (or unit eigenvector $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1, 1, 1)^T$), we have $\mathbf{X}\cdot\mathbf{v}_1 = 3a$, so $(\mathbf{X}\cdot\mathbf{v}_1)\mathbf{X} = 3a^2 \mathbf{v}_1$ and
   \[
   I(P)\mathbf{v}_1 = \left(\frac{11}{3} - 3\right) M a^2 \mathbf{v}_1 = \frac{2}{3} M a^2 \mathbf{v}_1.
   \]
   Thus $\lambda_1 = \frac{2}{3} M a^2$ is a non-degenerate (multiplicity $1$) eigenvalue with one-dimensional eigenspace spanned by $(1, 1, 1)^T$. (Physically, the axis through $P$ in direction $(1,1,1)$ passes directly through the centre of mass $G$, so the moment of inertia about it equals that about $G$, namely $\frac{2}{3} M a^2$.)

2. **In the plane orthogonal to $(1, 1, 1)^T$:**
   For any non-zero vector $\mathbf{v} = (v_1, v_2, v_3)^T$ satisfying $\mathbf{X}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0$, i.e.
   \[
   v_1 + v_2 + v_3 = 0,
   \]
   we immediately have
   \[
   I(P)\mathbf{v} = \frac{11}{3} M a^2 \mathbf{v}.
   \]
   Since this plane is two-dimensional, $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3 = \frac{11}{3} M a^2$ is a doubly degenerate (multiplicity $2$) eigenvalue whose eigenspace consists of all non-zero vectors $\mathbf{v} = (v_1, v_2, v_3)^T$ with $v_1 + v_2 + v_3 = 0$. For example, an orthogonal basis of this eigenspace is
   \[
   \mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{v}_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}
   \qquad \left(\text{normalised: } \hat{\mathbf{e}}_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \; \hat{\mathbf{e}}_3 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\right).
   \]
   (Physically, any axis through $P$ perpendicular to $GP$ lies at perpendicular distance $d = |GP| = \sqrt{3}a$ from the parallel axis through $G$, giving moment of inertia $\frac{2}{3} M a^2 + M(\sqrt{3}a)^2 = \frac{11}{3} M a^2$.)

**Verification via characteristic polynomial:** Writing $I(P) = \frac{Ma^2}{3} A$,
\[
\det(A - \mu I)
= \det\begin{pmatrix} 8-\mu & -3 & -3 \\ -3 & 8-\mu & -3 \\ -3 & -3 & 8-\mu \end{pmatrix}
= (2-\mu) \det\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ -3 & 8-\mu & -3 \\ -3 & -3 & 8-\mu \end{pmatrix}
= (2-\mu)(11-\mu)^2,
\]
confirming the eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = \frac{2}{3} M a^2$ (multiplicity $1$) and $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3 = \frac{11}{3} M a^2$ (multiplicity $2$), with $\operatorname{tr}(I(P)) = 3\cdot\frac{8}{3} M a^2 = 8 M a^2 = \left(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{11}{3} + \frac{11}{3}\right) M a^2$.

## Final Answers

- **Parallel-axis theorem:** $I_{ij}(P) = I_{ij}(G) + M(X_k X_k \delta_{ij} - X_i X_j)$.
- **Inertia tensor about $G$:**
  \[
  I_{ij}(G) = \frac{2}{3} M a^2 \delta_{ij} \qquad (M = 8\rho a^3).
  \]
- **Inertia tensor about $P = (a,a,a)$:**
  \[
  I(P) = M a^2 \begin{pmatrix} \frac{8}{3} & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & \frac{8}{3} & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & \frac{8}{3} \end{pmatrix} = \frac{M a^2}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 8 & -3 & -3 \\ -3 & 8 & -3 \\ -3 & -3 & 8 \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
- **Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $I_{ij}(P)$:**
  - $\lambda_1 = \dfrac{2}{3} M a^2$ (multiplicity $1$): eigenspace spanned by $(1, 1, 1)^T$;
  - $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3 = \dfrac{11}{3} M a^2$ (multiplicity $2$): eigenspace $\{(v_1, v_2, v_3)^T \in \mathbb{R}^3 : v_1 + v_2 + v_3 = 0\}$ (e.g. spanned by $(1, -1, 0)^T$ and $(1, 1, -2)^T$).
