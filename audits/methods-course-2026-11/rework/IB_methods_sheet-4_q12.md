## Full Solution

### 1. Problem statement and sign convention

Consistent with Question 7 on the same sheet, for a fixed source point $\mathbf{y} = (y_1,\dots,y_n) \in \Omega$, the Dirichlet Green's function $G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})$ for the Laplacian on the positive orthant
\[
\Omega = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n : x_1 > 0, \dots, x_n > 0\}
\]
satisfies
\[
\begin{cases}
\Delta_{\mathbf{x}} G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = \delta(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}), & \mathbf{x} \in \Omega, \\
G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = 0, & \mathbf{x} \in \partial\Omega,
\end{cases}
\]
together with the decay/boundedness condition at infinity needed for uniqueness on the unbounded domain $\Omega$: $G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) \to 0$ as $|\mathbf{x}| \to \infty$ for $n \ge 2$ (and $G(x;y)$ bounded as $x \to \infty$ for $n = 1$). (Under the opposite convention $-\Delta G = \delta$, one simply negates $G$.)

### 2. Free-space fundamental solution $\Phi_n$ of the Laplacian in $\mathbb{R}^n$

First construct the radially symmetric fundamental solution $\Phi_n(\mathbf{r}) = \phi(r)$ ($r = |\mathbf{r}|$) satisfying $\Delta \Phi_n(\mathbf{r}) = \delta(\mathbf{r})$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$. For $r > 0$,
\[
\Delta \phi = \phi''(r) + \frac{n-1}{r}\phi'(r) = \frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\!\left(r^{n-1}\phi'(r)\right) = 0 \implies \phi'(r) = A r^{1-n}
\]
for a constant $A$. Integrating $\Delta \Phi_n = \delta$ over the ball $B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{0})$ of radius $\varepsilon > 0$ and applying the divergence theorem gives
\[
1 = \int_{B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{0})} \Delta \Phi_n\,\mathrm{d}^n\mathbf{r} = \int_{\partial B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{0})} \phi'(\varepsilon)\,\mathrm{d}S = A\varepsilon^{1-n}\cdot \omega_n \varepsilon^{n-1} = A\omega_n,
\]
where $\omega_n = \operatorname{Area}(S^{n-1})$ is the surface area of the unit $(n-1)$-sphere in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Evaluating the $n$-dimensional Gaussian integral in Cartesian and spherical polar coordinates determines $\omega_n$:
\[
\pi^{n/2} = \left(\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^n = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} e^{-|\mathbf{x}|^2}\,\mathrm{d}^n\mathbf{x} = \omega_n \int_0^\infty e^{-r^2} r^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}r = \frac{\omega_n}{2}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right),
\]
so $\omega_n = \frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}$ (giving $\omega_1 = 2$, $\omega_2 = 2\pi$, $\omega_3 = 4\pi$). Thus $A = 1/\omega_n$, and integrating $\phi'(r) = r^{1-n}/\omega_n$ yields (up to an irrelevant additive constant that cancels in the alternating sum below):
\[
\Phi_n(\mathbf{r}) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{r}|, & n = 1, \\[8pt]
\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\mathbf{r}|, & n = 2, \\[8pt]
-\dfrac{1}{(n-2)\omega_n |\mathbf{r}|^{n-2}}, & n \ge 3.
\end{cases}
\]

### 3. Construction by the method of images

The boundary $\partial\Omega$ is the union of the $n$ coordinate hyperplane faces
\[
\partial\Omega = \bigcup_{k=1}^n \Gamma_k, \qquad \Gamma_k = \{\mathbf{x} \in \overline{\Omega} : x_k = 0\}.
\]
The reflection group generated by reflections across the $n$ coordinate hyperplanes $x_k = 0$ ($k = 1,\dots,n$) is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^n$ and has order $2^n$. Each group element is indexed by a sign vector
\[
\boldsymbol{\sigma} = (\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n) \in \{-1, +1\}^n.
\]
For a fixed source $\mathbf{y} = (y_1, \dots, y_n) \in \Omega$ and each $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in \{-1,+1\}^n$, define the reflected point
\[
\mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} = (\sigma_1 y_1, \sigma_2 y_2, \dots, \sigma_n y_n),
\]
and assign it the image charge (parity)
\[
\operatorname{sgn}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) = \prod_{j=1}^n \sigma_j \in \{-1, +1\}
\]
(i.e. $(-1)^{|I|}$ where $I = \{j : \sigma_j = -1\}$ is the set of reflected coordinates: every reflection across a coordinate hyperplane reverses the sign of the image charge).

We construct the Dirichlet Green's function as the alternating sum over all $2^n$ points (the original source at $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = \mathbf{1} := (+1,\dots,+1)$ plus the $2^n - 1$ image points):
\[
G(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in \{-1, 1\}^n} \left(\prod_{j=1}^n \sigma_j\right) \Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}).
\]
Explicitly:
- For $n = 1$ ($\Omega = (0,\infty)$):
  \[
  G(x;y) = \frac{1}{2}|x - y| - \frac{1}{2}|x + y| = -\min(x,y).
  \]
- For $n = 2$ ($\Omega = \{x_1 > 0, x_2 > 0\}$):
  \[
  G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|\mathbf{x} - (y_1, y_2)|\,|\mathbf{x} - (-y_1, -y_2)|}{|\mathbf{x} - (-y_1, y_2)|\,|\mathbf{x} - (y_1, -y_2)|}.
  \]
- For $n \ge 3$:
  \[
  G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = -\frac{1}{(n-2)\omega_n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in \{-1,1\}^n} \left(\prod_{j=1}^n \sigma_j\right) \frac{1}{|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|^{n-2}}, \qquad \omega_n = \frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}.
  \]

### 4. Verification of all Green's function properties

Fix $\mathbf{y} \in \Omega$, so $y_j > 0$ for all $j = 1, \dots, n$.

1. **PDE $\Delta_{\mathbf{x}} G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = \delta(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$ in $\Omega$**:
   - For $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = \mathbf{1} = (+1,\dots,+1)$, we have $\mathbf{y}_{\mathbf{1}} = \mathbf{y} \in \Omega$ and $\prod_{j=1}^n \sigma_j = +1$, contributing $\Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$ with $\Delta_{\mathbf{x}} \Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}) = \delta(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$ in $\Omega$.
   - For any $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \neq \mathbf{1}$, there exists at least one index $k$ with $\sigma_k = -1$, so $(\mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})_k = -y_k < 0$. Hence $\mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \notin \overline{\Omega}$ (in fact, $|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}| \ge x_k + y_k \ge y_k > 0$ for all $\mathbf{x} \in \overline{\Omega}$). Therefore $\Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})$ is smooth on an open neighbourhood of $\overline{\Omega}$ and harmonic in $\Omega$: $\Delta_{\mathbf{x}} \Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}) = 0$ for $\mathbf{x} \in \Omega$.

   By linearity of $\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}$,
   \[
   \Delta_{\mathbf{x}} G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = \Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\Phi_n(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}) + \sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\neq \mathbf{1}} \left(\prod_{j=1}^n \sigma_j\right) \underbrace{\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\Phi_n(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}_{=\,0} = \delta(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}), \qquad \mathbf{x} \in \Omega.
   \]

2. **Homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition $G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$**:
   Let $\mathbf{x} \in \partial\Omega$. Then $x_j \ge 0$ for all $j$, and there exists at least one $k \in \{1,\dots,n\}$ such that $x_k = 0$. Since $|(\mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})_k| = y_k > 0$, we have $\mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ for every $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, so every term in $G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})$ is finite.

   Define the fixed-point-free involution $\tau_k : \{-1,1\}^n \to \{-1,1\}^n$ that negates the $k$-th entry:
   \[
   (\tau_k \boldsymbol{\sigma})_j = \begin{cases} -\sigma_k, & j = k, \\ \sigma_j, & j \neq k. \end{cases}
   \]
   Then for every $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in \{-1,1\}^n$:
   - $\prod_{j=1}^n (\tau_k\boldsymbol{\sigma})_j = -\prod_{j=1}^n \sigma_j$;
   - Because $x_k = 0$,
     \[
     |\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\tau_k\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|^2 = (0 + \sigma_k y_k)^2 + \sum_{j\neq k}(x_j - \sigma_j y_j)^2 = (0 - \sigma_k y_k)^2 + \sum_{j\neq k}(x_j - \sigma_j y_j)^2 = |\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|^2,
     \]
     and since $\Phi_n(\mathbf{r})$ depends only on $|\mathbf{r}|$, $\Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\tau_k\boldsymbol{\sigma}}) = \Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})$.

   Pairing each $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ with $\sigma_k = +1$ against $\tau_k\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ (which has $\sigma_k = -1$) gives exact pairwise cancellation:
   \[
   G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = \sum_{\substack{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\{-1,1\}^n \\ \sigma_k = +1}} \left(\prod_{j=1}^n \sigma_j\right) \Bigl[\Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}) - \Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\tau_k\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\Bigr] = 0.
   \]
   This holds on every face $\Gamma_k$, as well as on all edges and at the corner $\mathbf{0}$.

3. **Symmetry**:
   Since $\sigma_j^2 = 1$, $(x_j - \sigma_j y_j)^2 = \sigma_j^2(x_j - \sigma_j y_j)^2 = (\sigma_j x_j - y_j)^2$, so $|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}| = |\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{x}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|$ for every $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$. Because $\Phi_n$ is radial, $G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = G(\mathbf{y};\mathbf{x})$ for all $\mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{y}$ in $\Omega$.

4. **Behaviour as $|\mathbf{x}| \to \infty$ (and uniqueness)**:
   - For $n = 1$, $G(x;y) = -y$ for $x > y$, which is bounded as $x \to \infty$.
   - For $n = 2$, dividing numerator and denominator by $|\mathbf{x}|^2$ gives
     \[
     G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\left|\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \frac{\mathbf{y}_{(+,+)}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\right|\left|\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \frac{\mathbf{y}_{(-,-)}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\right|}{\left|\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \frac{\mathbf{y}_{(-,+)}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\right|\left|\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \frac{\mathbf{y}_{(+,-)}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\right|} \longrightarrow \frac{1}{2\pi}\ln 1 = 0 \qquad \text{as } |\mathbf{x}| \to \infty
     \]
     (more precisely, $G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = -\frac{4 x_1 x_2 y_1 y_2}{\pi |\mathbf{x}|^4} + O(|\mathbf{x}|^{-4})$).
   - For $n \ge 3$, every term $|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}|^{2-n} \to 0$ as $|\mathbf{x}| \to \infty$ (in fact, because the alternating sum is a mixed $n$-th difference—a $2^n$-pole—$G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = O(|\mathbf{x}|^{2-2n})$ as $|\mathbf{x}| \to \infty$).

   Notice that on the unbounded orthant $\Omega$, without imposing decay ($G \to 0$ as $|\mathbf{x}| \to \infty$ for $n \ge 2$, or boundedness for $n = 1$), $G$ would only be unique up to adding harmonic functions vanishing on $\partial\Omega$ such as $c\, x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n$. With this decay/boundedness condition, the difference $h = G_1 - G_2$ of any two Dirichlet Green's functions is harmonic on $\Omega$, vanishes on $\partial\Omega$, and vanishes at infinity (for $n \ge 2$), so $h \equiv 0$ by the maximum principle on $\Omega \cap B_R(\mathbf{0})$ as $R \to \infty$.

## Final Answers

With the sheet's convention $\Delta_{\mathbf{x}} G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = \delta(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$:
\[
G(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in \{-1, 1\}^n} \left(\prod_{j=1}^n \sigma_j\right) \Phi_n(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}),
\]
where $\mathbf{y}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} = (\sigma_1 y_1, \dots, \sigma_n y_n)$, $\omega_n = \frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}$, and the free-space fundamental solution is
\[
\Phi_n(\mathbf{r}) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{r}|, & n = 1 \quad\bigl(\text{giving } G(x;y) = -\min(x,y)\bigr), \\[8pt]
\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\mathbf{r}|, & n = 2, \\[8pt]
-\dfrac{1}{(n-2)\omega_n |\mathbf{r}|^{n-2}}, & n \ge 3.
\end{cases}
\]
