## Full Solution

### 1. Definition of Convolution and Proof of the Convolution Theorem

Given two functions $f, g : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$ (for which the integral exists, e.g. $f, g \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$), their **convolution** $f * g$ is defined by
\[
(f * g)(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(y)\,g(x - y)\,dy = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x - y)\,g(y)\,dy.
\]

Using the given Fourier transform convention $\tilde{f}(k) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ikx} f(x)\,dx$, we compute:
\[
\widetilde{f * g}\,(k)
= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ikx}\!\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(y)\,g(x - y)\,dy\right)dx.
\]
Interchanging the order of integration (justified by Fubini's theorem when $f, g \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$):
\[
\widetilde{f * g}\,(k)
= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(y)\!\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ikx}\,g(x - y)\,dx\right)dy.
\]
Making the substitution $u = x - y$ in the inner integral (with $y$ fixed, so $x = u + y$, $dx = du$, and the limits remain $-\infty$ to $\infty$):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde{f * g}\,(k)
&= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(y)\!\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ik(u+y)}\,g(u)\,du\right)dy \\
&= \left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-iky}\,f(y)\,dy\right)\!\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-iku}\,g(u)\,du\right) \\
&= \tilde{f}(k)\,\tilde{g}(k).
\end{aligned}
\]

### 2. Dual Convolution Theorem (Transform of a Product)

With the given convention, the Fourier inversion formula is
\[
f(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{iqx}\,\tilde{f}(q)\,dq.
\]
For a product $h(x) = f(x)\,g(x)$, substituting the inversion formula for $f$ (or applying the convolution theorem to the inverse Fourier transform) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{h}(k) = \widetilde{fg}\,(k)
&= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ikx}\!\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{iqx}\,\tilde{f}(q)\,dq\right)g(x)\,dx \\
&= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{f}(q)\!\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-i(k-q)x}\,g(x)\,dx\right)dq \\
&= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{f}(q)\,\tilde{g}(k - q)\,dq \\
&= \frac{1}{2\pi}(\tilde{f} * \tilde{g})(k).
\end{aligned}
\]
(Note: As with $f(x) = 1/x$, which is not in $L^1(\mathbb{R})$, both $\mathcal{F}\{1/x\}$ and $\mathcal{F}\{\sin(x)/x^2\}$ are understood in the Cauchy principal value / tempered distribution sense; we verify directly below that the resulting formula also matches the explicit Cauchy principal value integral.)

---

### 3. Finding the Fourier Transform of $h(x) = \sin(x)/x^2$

Factor $h(x)$ as the product
\[
h(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x^2} = f(x)\,g(x), \qquad \text{where } f(x) = \frac{1}{x} \text{ and } g(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x}.
\]
We are given $\tilde{f}(k) = -i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(k)$.

#### Step 3a: Fourier transform of $g(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x}$

Write $g(x)$ using Euler's formula:
\[
g(x) = \frac{e^{ix} - e^{-ix}}{2i}\cdot\frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{2i}\big(e^{ix}f(x) - e^{-ix}f(x)\big).
\]
By the frequency-shift property $\mathcal{F}\{e^{iax}f(x)\}(k) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-i(k-a)x}f(x)\,dx = \tilde{f}(k - a)$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{g}(k)
&= \frac{1}{2i}\big(\tilde{f}(k - 1) - \tilde{f}(k + 1)\big) \\
&= \frac{1}{2i}\big(-i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(k - 1) + i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(k + 1)\big) \\
&= \frac{\pi}{2}\big(\operatorname{sgn}(k + 1) - \operatorname{sgn}(k - 1)\big).
\end{aligned}
\]
Evaluating by cases:
\[
\tilde{g}(k) = \begin{cases}
\pi, & |k| < 1, \\
0, & |k| > 1
\end{cases}
\]
(with value $\pi/2$ at the jump points $k = \pm 1$; equivalently, direct Fourier inversion verifies $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1} \pi e^{ikx}\,dk = \frac{e^{ix} - e^{-ix}}{2ix} = \frac{\sin x}{x}$).

#### Step 3b: Evaluating $\tilde{h}(k) = \frac{1}{2\pi}(\tilde{g} * \tilde{f})(k)$

By commutativity of convolution and the dual convolution theorem,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{h}(k)
&= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{g}(q)\,\tilde{f}(k - q)\,dq \\
&= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1} \pi \big(-i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(k - q)\big)\,dq \\
&= -\frac{i\pi}{2}\int_{-1}^{1} \operatorname{sgn}(k - q)\,dq.
\end{aligned}
\]
We evaluate the integral in three regions:

1. **$k \ge 1$**: For all $q \in (-1, 1)$, $k - q > 0$, so $\operatorname{sgn}(k - q) = +1$:
   \[
   \int_{-1}^{1} \operatorname{sgn}(k - q)\,dq = \int_{-1}^{1} 1\,dq = 2
   \implies \tilde{h}(k) = -\frac{i\pi}{2}(2) = -i\pi.
   \]
2. **$k \le -1$**: For all $q \in (-1, 1)$, $k - q < 0$, so $\operatorname{sgn}(k - q) = -1$:
   \[
   \int_{-1}^{1} \operatorname{sgn}(k - q)\,dq = \int_{-1}^{1} (-1)\,dq = -2
   \implies \tilde{h}(k) = -\frac{i\pi}{2}(-2) = i\pi.
   \]
3. **$-1 < k < 1$**: Splitting at $q = k$, we have $\operatorname{sgn}(k - q) = +1$ for $q \in (-1, k)$ and $\operatorname{sgn}(k - q) = -1$ for $q \in (k, 1)$:
   \[
   \int_{-1}^{1} \operatorname{sgn}(k - q)\,dq = (k - (-1)) - (1 - k) = 2k
   \implies \tilde{h}(k) = -\frac{i\pi}{2}(2k) = -i\pi k.
   \]

Notice $\tilde{h}(k)$ is continuous at $k = \pm 1$, odd, and purely imaginary (as expected for a real odd function $h(x)$). We may write the answer in piecewise or compact form:
\[
\tilde{h}(k) = \begin{cases}
i\pi, & k \le -1, \\
-i\pi k, & -1 \le k \le 1, \\
-i\pi, & k \ge 1,
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\tilde{h}(k) = -\frac{i\pi}{2}\big(|k + 1| - |k - 1|\big).
\]

#### Independent Verification via Cauchy Principal Value Integral

Since $h(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x^2}$ is odd, the even cosine part $\cos(kx)\frac{\sin x}{x^2}$ has zero Cauchy principal value, leaving an ordinary absolutely convergent integral:
\[
\tilde{h}(k) = \operatorname{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ikx}\frac{\sin x}{x^2}\,dx = -2i\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(kx)\sin x}{x^2}\,dx.
\]
Using $2\sin(kx)\sin x = \cos((k-1)x) - \cos((k+1)x)$ and integrating by parts (the boundary term $-\frac{\cos((k-1)x) - \cos((k+1)x)}{x}$ vanishes as $x \to 0^+$ since the numerator is $2kx^2 + O(x^4)$, and vanishes as $x \to \infty$):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(kx)\sin x}{x^2}\,dx
&= \frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{-(k-1)\sin((k-1)x) + (k+1)\sin((k+1)x)}{x}\,dx \\
&= \frac{\pi}{4}\big(|k + 1| - |k - 1|\big),
\end{aligned}
\]
using $\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(\omega x)}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)$. Multiplying by $-2i$ reproduces $\tilde{h}(k) = -\frac{i\pi}{2}(|k+1| - |k-1|)$ identically. (Likewise, $\frac{d\tilde{h}}{dk} = -i\pi\mathbf{1}_{|k|<1} = -i\tilde{g}(k) = \mathcal{F}\{-ix\,h(x)\}(k)$ with $\tilde{h}(0) = 0$ confirms the result immediately.)

## Final Answers

- **Convolution**: $(f * g)(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(y)\,g(x - y)\,dy$.
- **Convolution theorem**: $\widetilde{f * g}\,(k) = \tilde{f}(k)\,\tilde{g}(k)$.
- **Fourier transform of $\sin(x)/x^2$**:
  \[
  \mathcal{F}\!\left\{\frac{\sin x}{x^2}\right\}\!(k)
  = \begin{cases}
  i\pi, & k \le -1, \\
  -i\pi k, & -1 \le k \le 1, \\
  -i\pi, & k \ge 1,
  \end{cases}
  = -\frac{i\pi}{2}\big(|k + 1| - |k - 1|\big).
  \]
