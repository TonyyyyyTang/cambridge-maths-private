## Question (verbatim question.tex)
```latex
The function $u(x,y)$ satisfies Laplace’s equation in the half-space $y\geqslant0$, together with boundary conditions
\[\begin{aligned}u(x,y)&\to0\text{ as }y\to\infty\text{ for all }x,\\u(x,0)&=u_0(x),\quad\text{where }x\,u_0(x)\to0\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{aligned}\]
Using Fourier transforms, show that
\[u(x,y)=\int_{-\infty}^\infty u_0(t)v(x-t,y)dt,\]
where
\[v(x,y)=\frac{y}{\pi(x^2+y^2)}.\]
Suppose that $u_0(x)=(x^2+a^2)^{-1}.$ Using contour integration and the convolution theorem, or otherwise, show that
\[u(x,y)=\frac{y+a}{a\,[x^2+(y+a)^2]}.\]

[\emph{You may assume the convolution theorem of Fourier transforms, i.e. that if $\tilde f(k),\tilde g(k)$ are the Fourier transforms of two functions $f(x),g(x)$},\emph{ then $\tilde f(k)\tilde g(k)$ is the Fourier transform of $\int_{-\infty}^\infty f(t)g(x-t)dt.$}]
```
