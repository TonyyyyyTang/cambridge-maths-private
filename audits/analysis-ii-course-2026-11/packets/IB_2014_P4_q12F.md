## Question (verbatim question.tex)
```latex
Let $U\subset\mathbb R^2$ be an open set. Define what it means for a function $f:U\to\mathbb R$ to be \emph{differentiable} at a point $(x_0,y_0)\in U$.

Prove that if the partial derivatives $D_1f$ and $D_2f$ exist on $U$ and are continuous at $(x_0,y_0)$, then $f$ is differentiable at $(x_0,y_0)$.

If $f$ is differentiable on $U$ must $D_1f$, $D_2f$ be continuous at $(x_0,y_0)$? Give a proof or counterexample as appropriate.

The function $h:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R$ is defined by
\[
h(x,y)=xy\sin(1/x)\quad\text{for }x\ne0,\qquad h(0,y)=0.
\]
Determine all the points $(x,y)$ at which $h$ is differentiable.
```
