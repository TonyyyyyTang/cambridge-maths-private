## Question (verbatim question.tex)
```latex
Carefully state the inverse function theorem.

Let $\Omega$ be a bounded open subset of $\R^n$; denote its closure in $\R^n$ by $\overline\Omega$ and its boundary in $\R^n$ by $\partial\Omega=\overline\Omega\setminus\Omega$. Let $f:\overline\Omega\to\R^n$ be continuous in $\overline\Omega$ and $C^1$ in $\Omega$. Suppose $0\notin f(\partial\Omega)$ and that $\det Df(x)\ne0$ for every $x\in f^{-1}(0)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Show that the zeros of $f$ are isolated, i.e. that
\[
\forall x\in f^{-1}(0)\quad\exists r>0\text{ such that }
y\in B_r(x)\setminus\{x\}\implies f(y)\ne0.
\]
\item[(ii)] Recall that $\Omega$ is bounded and $0\notin f(\partial\Omega)$. Show that the zero set of $f$ is finite and contained in $\Omega$, i.e. $f^{-1}(0)=\{x_1,\ldots,x_N\}$ for some $N<\infty$ and $x_1,\ldots,x_N\in\Omega$. [You may assume results from lectures if properly stated.]

Deduce that there exist $r_1,\ldots,r_N>0$ such that
\[
\sup_{x\in B_{r_k}(x_k)}
\|[Df(x_k)]^{-1}[Df(x)-Df(x_k)]\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\frac14\quad\forall k=1,\ldots,N,
\]
where $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ is the operator norm on $\R^{n\times n}$ induced by a choice of norm $\|\cdot\|$ on $\R^n$.
\item[(iii)] Define $N(f)=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(0)}\operatorname{sign}(\det Df(x))$. Here, as usual, $\operatorname{sign}:\R\setminus\{0\}\to\{-1,1\}$ takes the value $+1$ for positive real numbers and $-1$ for negative real numbers.

Show that there exists $\delta>0$ sufficiently small such that, for all $g:\overline\Omega\to\R^n$ continuous in $\overline\Omega$ and $C^1$ in $\Omega$,
\[
\sup_\Omega\|g-f\|+\sup_\Omega\|Dg-Df\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\delta
\implies N(g)=N(f).
\]
\end{enumerate}
[You may assume the following statement. Let $r>0$, $x\in\R^n$ and $h:\overline{B_r(x)}\to\R^n$ be continuous in $\overline{B_r(x)}$ and $C^1$ in $B_r(x)$. If both $\sup_{B_r(x)}\|A^{-1}(Dh-A)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac12$ and $\sup_{B_r(x)}\|A^{-1}h\|\le\frac r2$ for some invertible $A\in\R^{n\times n}$, then $h$ has a unique zero in $B_r(x)$ and $\operatorname{sign}(\det Dh(y))=\operatorname{sign}(\det A)$ for all $y\in B_r(x)$.]
```
