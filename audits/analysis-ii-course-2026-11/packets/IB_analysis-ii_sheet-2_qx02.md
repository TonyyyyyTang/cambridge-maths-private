## Question (verbatim question.tex)
```latex
Let $(X,d)$ and $(Y,d')$ be metric spaces, and $f:X\to Y$ be a map. Recall from lectures the following definition of continuity: we say $f$ is continuous at $x\in X$ if
\[
\forall\epsilon>0,\exists\delta=\delta(x,\epsilon)>0\text{ such that }d(x,y)<\delta\implies d'(f(x),f(y)<\epsilon.
\]
(a) Show that the following is an equivalent definition: $f$ is continuous at $x$ if $\forall V\subset Y$ which is a neighborhood of $f(x)$, $U=f^{-1}(V)$ is a neighborhood of $x$. This second, more abstract, definition will come in handy soon!

(b) Deduce that $f$ is continuous in $X$ if and only if $\forall V\subset Y$ open in $Y$, $f^{-1}(V)$ is open in $X$.

(c) Show that $f$ is continuous in $X$ if and only if $\forall V\subset Y$ closed in $Y$, $f^{-1}(V)$ is closed in $X$.
```
