## Question (verbatim question.tex)
```latex
Let $(X_n)_{n\ge0}$ be a Markov chain with transition matrix $P$. What is a \emph{stopping time} of $(X_n)_{n\ge0}$? What is the \emph{strong Markov property}?

The exciting game of ‘Unopoly’ is played by a single player on a board of $4$ squares. The player starts with £$m$ (where $m\in\N$). During each turn, the player tosses a fair coin and moves one or two places in a clockwise direction ($1\to2\to3\to4\to1$) according to whether the coin lands heads or tails respectively. The player collects £2 each time they pass (or land on) square $1$. If the player lands on square $3$ however, they immediately lose £1 and go back to square $2$. The game continues indefinitely unless the player is on square $2$ with £0, in which case the player loses the game and the game ends.

\diagram{unopoly-board}{Four-square Unopoly board from the examination paper.}

(a) By setting up an appropriate Markov chain, show that if the player is at square $2$ with £$m$, where $m\ge1$, the probability that they are ever at square $2$ with £$(m-1)$ is $2/3$.

(b) Find the probability of losing the game when the player starts on square $1$ with £$m$, where $m\ge1$.

[\emph{Hint: Take the state space of your Markov chain to be $\{1,2,4\}\times\{0,1,\ldots\}$.}]
```
