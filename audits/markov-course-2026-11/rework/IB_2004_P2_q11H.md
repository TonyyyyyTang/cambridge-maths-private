## Full Solution

### 1. Definition of Reversibility

Let $(X_r)_{r\geqslant 0}$ be a Markov chain on $S$ with transition matrix $P = (P_{ij})$ and initial distribution $P(X_0 = i) = \pi_i$ ($i \in S$), where $\pi = (\pi_i)_{i\in S}$ is the unique invariant distribution (so $P(X_r = i) = \pi_i$ for all $r \geqslant 0$ and $i \in S$).

The Markov chain $(X_r)_{r\geqslant 0}$ is said to be **reversible** if, for every integer $N \geqslant 1$ (equivalently, every $N \geqslant 0$), the time-reversed process $(X_{N-r})_{0 \leqslant r \leqslant N}$ has the same joint distribution as $(X_r)_{0 \leqslant r \leqslant N}$: that is, for all $N \geqslant 0$ and all $i_0, i_1, \dots, i_N \in S$,
\[
P(X_N = i_0, X_{N-1} = i_1, \dots, X_0 = i_N) = P(X_0 = i_0, X_1 = i_1, \dots, X_N = i_N).
\]
Equivalently, for every $N \geqslant 1$, the time-reversed process $(X_{N-r})_{0 \leqslant r \leqslant N}$ is a $\operatorname{Markov}(\pi, P)$ chain.

*(Remark on positivity of $\pi$: Since $(X_r)_{r\geqslant 0}$ is irreducible and positive-recurrent, $\pi_i > 0$ for every $i \in S$. Indeed, $\sum_{k\in S} \pi_k = 1$ implies $\pi_k > 0$ for some $k \in S$; for any $i \in S$, irreducibility gives $m \geqslant 0$ with $(P^m)_{ki} > 0$, whence $\pi_i = (\pi P^m)_i = \sum_{j\in S} \pi_j (P^m)_{ji} \geqslant \pi_k (P^m)_{ki} > 0$. This ensures conditional probabilities given $\{X_r = i\}$ are everywhere well-defined, though the finite-dimensional proof below holds even without dividing by $\pi_i$.)*

### 2. Proof of the Equivalence

We prove that $(X_r)_{r\geqslant 0}$ is reversible if and only if the **detailed balance equations**
\[
\pi_i P_{ij} = \pi_j P_{ji} \qquad \text{for all } i, j \in S
\]
hold.

#### ($\Rightarrow$) Necessity
Suppose $(X_r)_{r\geqslant 0}$ is reversible. Taking $N = 1$ in the definition of reversibility, for any $i, j \in S$ we have
\[
P(X_0 = i, X_1 = j) = P(X_1 = i, X_0 = j) = P(X_0 = j, X_1 = i).
\]
Since $(X_r)_{r\geqslant 0}$ is $\operatorname{Markov}(\pi, P)$, the left-hand side equals $P(X_0 = i) P(X_1 = j \mid X_0 = i) = \pi_i P_{ij}$, and the right-hand side equals $P(X_0 = j) P(X_1 = i \mid X_0 = j) = \pi_j P_{ji}$. Hence
\[
\pi_i P_{ij} = \pi_j P_{ji} \qquad \text{for all } i, j \in S.
\]

#### ($\Leftarrow$) Sufficiency
Suppose $\pi_i P_{ij} = \pi_j P_{ji}$ for all $i, j \in S$. We prove by induction on $N \geqslant 0$ that for all $i_0, i_1, \dots, i_N \in S$,
\[
\pi_{i_N} P_{i_N, i_{N-1}} P_{i_{N-1}, i_{N-2}} \cdots P_{i_1, i_0}
= \pi_{i_0} P_{i_0, i_1} P_{i_1, i_2} \cdots P_{i_{N-1}, i_N}.
\]
- **Base case ($N = 0$):** Both sides equal $\pi_{i_0}$.
- **Inductive step:** Assume the identity holds for $N - 1$ (where $N \geqslant 1$). Then, for any $i_0, i_1, \dots, i_N \in S$, applying detailed balance to the pair $(i_N, i_{N-1})$ gives $\pi_{i_N} P_{i_N, i_{N-1}} = \pi_{i_{N-1}} P_{i_{N-1}, i_N}$, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_{i_N} P_{i_N, i_{N-1}} P_{i_{N-1}, i_{N-2}} \cdots P_{i_1, i_0}
&= P_{i_{N-1}, i_N} \left(\pi_{i_{N-1}} P_{i_{N-1}, i_{N-2}} \cdots P_{i_1, i_0}\right) \\
&= P_{i_{N-1}, i_N} \left(\pi_{i_0} P_{i_0, i_1} \cdots P_{i_{N-2}, i_{N-1}}\right) \quad \text{(by the induction hypothesis)} \\
&= \pi_{i_0} P_{i_0, i_1} \cdots P_{i_{N-2}, i_{N-1}} P_{i_{N-1}, i_N}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This completes the induction.

Now, for any $N \geqslant 0$ and any $i_0, i_1, \dots, i_N \in S$, since $(X_r)_{r\geqslant 0}$ is $\operatorname{Markov}(\pi, P)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(X_N = i_0, X_{N-1} = i_1, \dots, X_0 = i_N)
&= P(X_0 = i_N, X_1 = i_{N-1}, \dots, X_N = i_0) \\
&= \pi_{i_N} P_{i_N, i_{N-1}} P_{i_{N-1}, i_{N-2}} \cdots P_{i_1, i_0} \\
&= \pi_{i_0} P_{i_0, i_1} \cdots P_{i_{N-1}, i_N} \\
&= P(X_0 = i_0, X_1 = i_1, \dots, X_N = i_N).
\end{aligned}
\]
Because the joint distribution of $(X_{N-r})_{0\leqslant r\leqslant N}$ agrees with that of $(X_r)_{0\leqslant r\leqslant N}$ for every $N \geqslant 0$, the chain $(X_r)_{r\geqslant 0}$ is reversible. $\square$

*(Alternative formulation via the time-reversed transition matrix: Since $\pi_i > 0$ for all $i \in S$, one may define $\hat{P}_{ij} = \frac{\pi_j}{\pi_i} P_{ji}$. Because $\pi P = \pi$, $\hat{P}$ is stochastic ($\sum_j \hat{P}_{ij} = \frac{1}{\pi_i}\sum_j \pi_j P_{ji} = 1$), and the same telescoping calculation shows $P(X_N = i_0, \dots, X_0 = i_N) = \pi_{i_0}\hat{P}_{i_0 i_1}\cdots\hat{P}_{i_{N-1}i_N}$, i.e., $(X_{N-r})_{0\leqslant r\leqslant N}$ is $\operatorname{Markov}(\pi, \hat{P})$. Since $\pi_i > 0$ for all $i$, this has the same distribution as $\operatorname{Markov}(\pi, P)$ for all $N\geqslant 1$ if and only if $\hat{P}_{ij} = P_{ij}$ for all $i,j\in S$, i.e., $\pi_i P_{ij} = \pi_j P_{ji}$ for all $i,j\in S$.)*
