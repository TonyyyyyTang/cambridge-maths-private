### Part (a): Transition probabilities and reversibility in equilibrium

The state space is $S = \{0, 1, \dots, k\}$, where $X_n$ is the number of balls in box A at time $n$ (and hence there are $k - X_n$ balls in box B).

Given $X_n = i \in \{0, 1, \dots, k\}$, one of the $k$ balls is selected uniformly at random (each with probability $1/k$), independently of the past, and moved to the opposite box:
- With probability $(k - i)/k$, a ball currently in box B is chosen and moved to box A, so $X_{n+1} = i + 1$.
- With probability $i/k$, a ball currently in box A is chosen and moved to box B, so $X_{n+1} = i - 1$.

Thus the transition probabilities $p_{ij} = \mathbb{P}(X_{n+1} = j \mid X_n = i)$ for $i, j \in \{0, 1, \dots, k\}$ are
\[
p_{i, i+1} = \frac{k - i}{k} \quad (0 \le i \le k - 1), \qquad
p_{i, i-1} = \frac{i}{k} \quad (1 \le i \le k),
\]
and $p_{ij} = 0$ for all other pairs $(i, j)$. (At the boundary states, $p_{0, 1} = 1$ and $p_{k, k-1} = 1$; for every $i \in S$, $\sum_{j=0}^k p_{ij} = \frac{k-i}{k} + \frac{i}{k} = 1$.)

**Irreducibility and uniqueness of the equilibrium distribution.** Since $p_{i, i+1} > 0$ for all $0 \le i \le k - 1$ and $p_{i, i-1} > 0$ for all $1 \le i \le k$, every state communicates with every other state (from $i < j$, $p_{i,i+1}\cdots p_{j-1,j} > 0$, and symmetrically from $j$ to $i$). Hence $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ is an irreducible Markov chain on a finite state space, so it possesses a unique invariant (equilibrium) distribution $\pi$.

**Detailed balance and finding $\pi$.** We look for a probability distribution $\pi = (\pi_0, \pi_1, \dots, \pi_k)$ satisfying the detailed balance equations
\[
\pi_i p_{ij} = \pi_j p_{ji} \qquad \text{for all } i, j \in \{0, 1, \dots, k\}.
\]
For $|i - j| \ne 1$, both sides are $0$. For $j = i + 1$ with $0 \le i \le k - 1$, detailed balance requires
\[
\pi_i \frac{k - i}{k} = \pi_{i+1} \frac{i + 1}{k} \iff \pi_{i+1} = \frac{k - i}{i + 1}\pi_i.
\]
By induction on $i \in \{0, 1, \dots, k\}$,
\[
\pi_i = \frac{k(k-1)\cdots(k-i+1)}{1 \cdot 2 \cdots i}\pi_0 = \binom{k}{i}\pi_0.
\]
Normalising $\sum_{i=0}^k \pi_i = 1$ gives
\[
1 = \pi_0 \sum_{i=0}^k \binom{k}{i} = 2^k \pi_0 \implies \pi_0 = 2^{-k},
\]
so
\[
\pi_i = \binom{k}{i} 2^{-k}, \qquad i \in \{0, 1, \dots, k\} \qquad \left(\text{i.e., } \pi = \mathrm{Bin}\!\left(k, \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right).
\]
Explicitly checking detailed balance for $0 \le i \le k - 1$:
\[
\pi_i p_{i, i+1} = \binom{k}{i} 2^{-k} \frac{k-i}{k} = \frac{(k-1)!}{i!\,(k-1-i)!} 2^{-k} = \binom{k}{i+1} 2^{-k} \frac{i+1}{k} = \pi_{i+1} p_{i+1, i},
\]
and $\pi_i p_{ij} = \pi_j p_{ji} = 0$ for $|i - j| \ne 1$.

Summing detailed balance over $i \in S$ confirms that $\pi$ is an invariant distribution:
\[
(\pi P)_j = \sum_{i=0}^k \pi_i p_{ij} = \sum_{i=0}^k \pi_j p_{ji} = \pi_j \sum_{i=0}^k p_{ji} = \pi_j \qquad \text{for all } j \in S.
\]
By uniqueness, $\pi$ is *the* equilibrium distribution of the chain.

**Reversibility in equilibrium.** Since $P$ and $\pi$ are in detailed balance, the chain started in equilibrium ($X_0 \sim \pi$) is reversible: for any horizon $N \ge 0$ and any states $i_0, i_1, \dots, i_N \in S$, applying $\pi_a p_{ab} = \pi_b p_{ba}$ repeatedly yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{P}(X_0 = i_0, X_1 = i_1, \dots, X_N = i_N)
&= \pi_{i_0} p_{i_0 i_1} p_{i_1 i_2} \cdots p_{i_{N-1} i_N} \\
&= p_{i_1 i_0} \pi_{i_1} p_{i_1 i_2} \cdots p_{i_{N-1} i_N} \\
&= \cdots \\
&= p_{i_1 i_0} p_{i_2 i_1} \cdots p_{i_N i_{N-1}} \pi_{i_N} \\
&= \mathbb{P}(X_0 = i_N, X_1 = i_{N-1}, \dots, X_N = i_0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore the time-reversed chain $(X_{N-n})_{0 \le n \le N}$ has the same distribution as $(X_n)_{0 \le n \le N}$ (it is $\mathrm{Markov}(\pi, P)$), so $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ is reversible in equilibrium.

### Part (b): Expected return time $\mathbb{E}(T)$

We are given $X_0 = 0$ and $T = \inf\{n \ge 1 : X_n = 0\}$, which is the first return time $T_0$ to state $0$.

As established in part (a), $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ is an irreducible Markov chain on the finite state space $\{0, 1, \dots, k\}$, so it is positive recurrent with unique invariant distribution $\pi$ (satisfying $\pi_i > 0$ for all $i$). By the standard theorem relating the unique invariant distribution of an irreducible positive recurrent Markov chain to expected return times (specifically, the invariant measure $\gamma^{(0)}_j = \mathbb{E}_0\big[\sum_{n=0}^{T_0 - 1} \mathbf{1}_{\{X_n = j\}}\big]$ satisfies $\gamma^{(0)}_0 = 1$ and $\sum_{j=0}^k \gamma^{(0)}_j = \mathbb{E}_0[T_0]$, so by uniqueness of invariant measure up to scalar multiple, $\pi_j = \gamma^{(0)}_j / \mathbb{E}_0[T_0]$, giving $\pi_0 = 1/\mathbb{E}_0[T_0]$), we obtain
\[
\mathbb{E}(T) = \mathbb{E}_0[T_0] = \frac{1}{\pi_0} = \frac{1}{\binom{k}{0} 2^{-k}} = 2^k.
\]
